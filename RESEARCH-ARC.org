Terms used throughout, defined once here:
- *interaction field*: a per-mechanism spatial map formed by combining a ligand's and a
  receptor's modeled expression surfaces with a convolution operator.
- *convolution operator*: the function combining the two surfaces. The published pipeline
  uses the elementwise minimum; product, geometric mean and a kinetic (mass-action)
  operator are also implemented.
- *ELSA*: entropy-based local indicator of spatial association. Negatively oriented, so
  lower means more spatially coherent domains.
- *ARI*: adjusted Rand index, agreement between two partitions. 0 is chance, 1 identical.
- *CV predictive fit*: the calibrated measure built 2026-09-01. Scores a partition by how
  well it predicts held-out gene expression in spatial blocks it was not fitted on.
- *dominance*: fraction of locations at which the same partner supplies the minimum.

* The program in one sentence

Inferring spatial cell-cell signaling from transcriptomics involves a chain of modeling
choices (likelihood, input scale, convolution operator, spatial support, and uncertainty)
that are usually made silently and that materially change the answer. We make each one
explicit, probabilistic, and testable against an outcome that is about signaling rather
than about tissue architecture.

* Where the novelty is, and is not

Checked against the literature on 2026-09-07 (four web searches, not a systematic review).

*Not novel.* Clustering on ligand-receptor interaction scores to recover spatial niches is
a crowded field: SpaNiche, Renoir, LARIS, Niche-DE, COMMOT, MISTy, Squidpy, Giotto,
SpaTalk, and a 2026 benchmark of niche identification by domain segmentation. Most
directly, NICHES (Raredon et al., Bioinformatics 2023) already embeds ligand-receptor
signal proxies to find signaling niches in spatial data, using the product operator. That
is prior art from this lab and must be cited and distinguished explicitly.

*Also not novel.* Randomized feature-subset ablation is standard practice in the spatial
transcriptomics clustering literature, and feature-count saturation curves already exist;
one Genome Biology evaluation reports saturation near 700 reference spatially variable
genes. Our deconfounded depth sweep is a *correction* to our own published experiment, not
a new method, and should be written that way.

*Where the novelty actually is.* INLA-SPDE is mature in geostatistics (soil carbon,
species distribution, benthic ecology, spatio-temporal data fusion) and the searches
returned no application to ligand-receptor or cell-cell communication. Absence across four
queries is weak evidence, but it identifies the axis: a Bayesian geostatistical treatment,
with a real likelihood comparison and full posteriors, in a field that runs on point
estimates and imputation.

* Current paper (PONE-D-26-04349): what to do now

** Recommended: the honest minimum plus one uncertainty result

*Claim.* A probabilistic spatial framework for ligand-receptor interaction fields, in
which the uncertainty is propagated rather than discarded, and in which tissue
architecture is recoverable from the ensemble of fields.

*Novel data.* None. Everything is computed or an afternoon away.

*Work, in execution order.*
1. The five manuscript corrections (Fig 6 caption direction, the full-depth coherence
   overstatement, 1,449 rather than 1,477 distinct fields, Control B's clustering backend,
   and the already-fixed uncertainty sentence).
2. The scope statement: the fields estimate signaling *potential* under the standard
   co-expression assumption, not measured signaling activity.
3. Report Control A. The response letter already commits us to it.
4. Promote the deconfounded depth sweep, written as a correction to the published sweep.
5. Add the Jensen result: the pipeline computes min(E[L], E[R]) where the right quantity
   is E[min(L,R)]. The minimum is concave, so the published fields are biased upward, and
   most so where the posterior is widest. Posterior standard deviations were saved for
   every gene at every bin, so no refits are needed and there is a closed form for
   approximately Gaussian independent marginals.

*Firm exclusion.* The representation ladder must not go in. Its own reproduction of the
published configuration gives 23 domains and ARI 0.431 against our published labels, so it
did not reproduce the pipeline it claims to compare against.

** Considered and deferred: the full uncertainty reframe

Clustering over posterior draws to produce a distribution over partitions and a per-bin
assignment entropy map, plus credible intervals on domain count and ELSA, plus operator
sensitivity across the kinetic family. This is the better paper and the assignment entropy
map would be its best figure, but it changes the thesis mid-revision and the extension has
a deadline. It becomes Paper 3.

* Paper 2: what the convolution operator does, and whether it predicts signaling

*Target claim.* Ligand-receptor convolution operators are one physically motivated family
indexed by binding affinity rather than three arbitrary choices; operator and input scale
jointly determine whether pairing information survives; and the operator that best predicts
downstream pathway activity is not the one currently in use.

*Novel data.* None.

*What already exists.* =calc.conv()= implements min, product, geometric mean and a kinetic
operator solving the receptor-ligand equilibrium. Affinities k.d = 0.01, 0.5 and 1 were
computed on both observed counts and modeled estimates, each with and without ligand
diffusion: roughly 24 representations, none of them reported. As k.d approaches 0 the
kinetic operator converges to the minimum; as it grows large it converges to the product.
So the minimum and the product are the two limits of one curve.

*Experiments.*
1. Cluster and score every existing representation under CV predictive fit. This is the
   fair version of the representation ladder.
2. *Downstream target-gene validation, the backbone.* If a pathway is active somewhere its
   canonical targets should be elevated there. Roughly 27,590 genes were never used to
   build the fields. For well-characterized pathways (canonical Wnt: Axin2, Lef1, Nkd1,
   Notum, Sp5; Shh: Gli1, Ptch1, Hhip; Notch: Hes and Hey families; BMP: Id1-3; FGF: Spry,
   Dusp6), ask whether the interaction field predicts its own targets better than the
   ligand alone, the receptor alone, a linear combination of both, or a scrambled pairing.
   Control for the fact that targets also track tissue identity: compare against non-target
   gene sets matched on expression level and spatial coherence, and condition on domain.
3. A denser affinity sweep, with literature Kd values where they exist, asking where real
   tissue sits on the product-to-minimum continuum.
4. A rank or copula minimum as a scale-free arm, invariant to any monotone per-gene
   transform, which dissolves the raw-versus-standardized argument rather than settling it.
5. The one-line check of cor(L,R) for true pairs against dominance-matched random pairs.
   If true partners are more spatially correlated, that explains both "wrong direction"
   results in the matched-dominance scramble and converts a null into a positive finding.

*Why this goes first.* The assets exist, and experiment 2 is the only one in the program
whose outcome is about signaling rather than architecture. Without it every signaling claim
we make is unfalsifiable with the data at hand. A negative result is publishable and the
field should know it.

* Paper 3: uncertainty propagation through the pipeline

*Target claim.* Propagating posterior uncertainty changes the inferred architecture and its
confidence. Point-estimate pipelines are both biased (by Jensen's inequality) and
overconfident, and the bias is largest exactly where the data are thinnest.

*Novel data.* None. Standard deviations and 2.5/50/97.5 quantiles are saved for every gene
at every bin in the per-gene bru_prediction objects.

*Experiments.* Quantify the Jensen bias in magnitude and spatial pattern. Draw from the
posteriors, cluster each draw, and produce a distribution over partitions with a per-bin
assignment entropy map. Put credible intervals on domain count and on ELSA. Introduce
P(L > tau_L and R > tau_R | data) as a posterior co-presence operator: bounded in [0,1],
comparable across mechanisms by construction, and a statistical object rather than an
arithmetic one.

*Venue.* A statistics or statistical-methods journal rather than a biology one.

* Paper 4: a jointly specified multivariate field model

*Target claim.* Fitting 912 independent SPDE models discards the cross-gene spatial
correlation structure and gives the wrong joint uncertainty for any convolution of two
fields. A jointly specified multivariate model borrows strength and produces the correct
joint posterior.

*Novel data.* None.

*Experiments.* Implement a reduced-rank multivariate SPDE or coregionalization model.
Compare held-out predictive fit against the independent fits, especially at low sequencing
depth where borrowing strength should matter most. Show that the joint posterior changes
the convolution's uncertainty relative to the independence assumption Paper 3 relies on.

The most statistically ambitious of the set and the most work. Detail in section 1 of
NEXT-PAPER-DIRECTIONS.md.

* Paper 5: ligand diffusion and spatial asymmetry

*Target claim.* Paracrine signaling requires asymmetric spatial treatment, ligands acting
where they diffuse to and receptors where their cells are. Diffused ligand fields predict
downstream targets better, and fitted diffusion lengths recover published morphogen ranges.

*Novel data.* Not required. The diffused variants (m.est.d.*) already exist and the
diffusion code is in the repository. This was examined and deliberately excluded from the
current paper; the authors remain interested.

*Experiments.* Compare diffused against undiffused variants under the target-gene criterion
from Paper 2. Fit a diffusion length per ligand or per ligand class. Validate against
published gradient ranges for known morphogens (Shh, Wnt, Bmp, Fgf families).

*Where wet lab would help.* Immunostaining for ligand protein against its own mRNA on
adjacent sections would turn the fitted diffusion lengths from plausible to demonstrated.
Not required for a first version.

* Paper 6: developmental dynamics across stages

*Target claim.* Signaling field architecture reorganizes across embryonic stages in a
characterizable way, and the mechanisms whose fields change most identify the developmental
transitions.

*Novel data.* Probably none, but this needs checking before planning. Four timepoints sit
in =data-inputs/mouse-embryo/= (pre-fit-obj-1 through -4, with a mesh for each, 3.7 GB
total). *Nobody has confirmed that these four are the same modality, comparable in
sequencing depth, and processed identically.* Do that first.

*Experiments.* Fit the framework at each stage. Establish domain correspondence across
stages. Rank mechanisms by how much their fields change.

This is the biology paper rather than a methods paper, and it needs the methods settled
first, which is why it sits last.

* What would genuinely require new data

Only direct signaling validation: phospho-staining for pathway activation, reporter lines,
or spatial proteomics (CODEX, imaging mass cytometry, MIBI) on adjacent sections. That is
the capstone of the program and a separate grant. Paper 2's target-gene experiment is the
computational stand-in and is good enough to make the program falsifiable meanwhile.

Absolute cross-gene molar calibration would also require external data: molar spike-ins,
smFISH on a gene subset, or matched proteomics. See section 6.1 of
NEXT-PAPER-DIRECTIONS.md for why transcript counts cannot be converted to molar abundance
from this dataset alone, and why that asymmetry is the rigorous argument for per-gene
standardization.
