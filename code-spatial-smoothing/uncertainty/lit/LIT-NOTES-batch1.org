** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
The quantity is the amplitude field beta_k(s) of each task k on the cortical surface mesh (about 6,000 vertices per hemisphere after resampling in the HCP (Human Connectome Project) analysis), with a Matern Gaussian-field prior in SPDE (stochastic partial differential equation) form and fit by INLA (integrated nested Laplace approximations). Output is the joint posterior of each field. Uncertainty is used through the joint "posterior probability map" (PPM), i.e. the excursion function of Bolin and Lindgren: for threshold gamma, the positive excursion set E is the largest vertex set such that P(all vertices in the set exceed gamma) >= 1 - alpha. For a single subject with Gaussian likelihood the excursion function is computed as a mixture over INLA hyperparameter configurations of Gaussian excursion functions (sequential importance sampling of Bolin and Lindgren 2015). For group models they use Monte Carlo draws and the =excursions.mc= function (accepted manuscript, Section 3.1).

** How uncertainty is used to compare locations, regions, fields, conditions
- Compares each vertex against a fixed activation threshold gamma (0, 0.5 percent or 1 percent of baseline signal), not location against location. No differences-between-regions test is described in what I read.
- Joint rather than marginal: the joint PPM thresholded at 1 - alpha gives a set with joint exceedance probability at least 1 - alpha. The marginal PPM is described as requiring a multiple-comparisons correction.
- Comparison with classical GLM uses voxel-wise (vertex-wise) t tests with FDR (false discovery rate, Benjamini-Hochberg) and FWER (family-wise error rate, permutation) control.

** Spatial correlation, and evidence on ignoring it
- The Bayesian model puts spatial correlation in the prior (Matern SPDE). The classical comparator is "massive univariate": the paper states that "the classical GLM does not account for such dependence" (Section 1.1), with the correction for multiplicity handled by FDR or permutation FWER on the vertex tests.
- Evidence on what ignoring correlation does is limited to ONE simulated phantom. In arXiv v1 (Fig. 7 text, PDF p. 25-26; 46 by 55 phantom, 1256 voxels inside the brain mask, alpha = 0.01), false positive rate (FPR) and false negative rate (FNR) are printed under each panel, read from the PDF text. Activation 1: joint PPM FPR 0.56 percent, FNR 0.08 percent; marginal PPM FPR 1.11 percent, FNR 0.00 percent; FDR FPR 1.51 percent, FNR 1.59 percent; FWER FPR 1.35 percent, FNR 1.91 percent. Activation 2: joint PPM FPR 0.16 percent, FNR 0.88 percent; marginal PPM FPR 0.64 percent, FNR 0.48 percent; FDR FPR 0.48 percent, FNR 3.58 percent; FWER FPR 0.24 percent, FNR 3.74 percent. These are single-dataset proportions of truly inactive voxels flagged, not repeated-simulation type I error rates; the number of simulated datasets is not stated in what I read (it appears to be one). Marginal PPM flags about 2 to 4 times as many false positives as joint PPM here (ratios 1.11/0.56 and 0.64/0.16, computed by me), but the absolute rates are all small.

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: INLA SPDE fields plus the excursion-set machinery has been used for inference on spatially correlated imaging data in a statistics-journal paper, with joint posterior draws feeding =excursions.mc=. Supports the framing that marginal thresholding alone is a multiple-comparisons problem.
- Limits: (a) The joint-versus-marginal contrast is a threshold-calibration contrast, not a re-ranking: the authors report the two excursion functions are monotone transforms of each other, so ranking-based metrics (ROC) are identical and only the cutoff for "significant" differs (explained in Bolin and Lindgren; see the next two sections). (b) Evidence for false-positive control is one phantom with a Gaussian likelihood; ours is Poisson with an offset. (c) The threshold is a fixed level gamma, not a comparison between two fields. (d) The statement of "no multiple comparisons correction needed" should be read as "joint probability already accounts for it", for the specific event defined by the set.
- Caution on citing: the "identical ROC" wording differs between versions as noted; cite the accepted manuscript caption or the arXiv body text with the version stated.

* Bolin and Lindgren 2015, excursion and contour uncertainty regions

** Citation and what was read
Bolin D, Lindgren F (2015). "Excursion and contour uncertainty regions for latent Gaussian models." Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B 77(1): 85-106 (Crossref: online March 2014). doi:10.1111/rssb.12055.

Local text: =/var/home/aoz/data/a-s-omics-lit/bolin2015/arxiv.txt=. The JRSS-B typeset version was NOT accessed (paywalled, not tried beyond arXiv), and arXiv v1 is a 2012 preprint that predates two years of revision, so details may differ from the published paper. For example, the arXiv text says software is in "the supplementary material for the online version of the article", which I did not obtain.

** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
Input: a latent Gaussian model (LGM) posterior pi(x | y) for a field x(s) on a discretization (typically a Gaussian Markov random field from the SPDE approach). Output: (a) excursion sets E+_{u,alpha}(x) = the largest set D with P(D contained in {x > u}) >= 1 - alpha (Definition 2.3); (b) level-avoiding sets and contour uncertainty regions; (c) the excursion function F+_u(s) = sup{1 - alpha : s in E+_{u,alpha}}, which gives, for each location, the largest joint probability level at which that location is still in the set (Definition 2.6). Joint probabilities are computed with a sequential importance sampler for Gaussian integrals using the Cholesky factor of the precision matrix (Section 3.1.1).

** How uncertainty is used to compare locations, regions, fields
- Compares x(s) with a fixed level u. Joint, not pointwise: the marginal set D_m = {s : P(x(s) > u) >= 1 - alpha} (their Eq. 1) is explicitly called inadequate because "the confidence level alpha does not give us any information about the family-wise error rate".
- Multiplicity is handled by choosing the set so that the SIMULTANEOUS probability is 1 - alpha. With the one-parameter family (Definition 3.2) the method is a threshold on the marginal exceedance probability, "with the important difference that the correct joint ... posterior density is used when finding the threshold".
- Nested ordering and the parametric family (asked for explicitly). Algorithm 3.1 requires an increasing family D(rho) of candidate sets (D(rho1) contained in D(rho2) if rho1 < rho2); nodes are reordered by the order in which the family adds them and the joint probability P(D contained in {x > u}) is updated sequentially, stopping when it drops below 1 - alpha. The default one-parameter family (Definition 3.2) is built from marginal quantiles: D1+(rho) = {s : P(x(s) > u) >= 1 - rho}. In words: locations are added in decreasing order of their marginal exceedance probability. Bounds (Section 3.2.1): an upper bound U1 = {s : P(x(s) > u) >= 1 - alpha} (the marginal set, "the largest region D if x(s) is a perfectly correlated field") and a Bonferroni-type lower bound, with Holm-Bonferroni L2 being the largest set "if the stochastic variables x(si) are independent"; "if the variables are not independent or perfectly correlated, one has L2 contained in D contained in U1".
- Consequence of this family (inference from the definitions, consistent with Mejia et al. 2020): the joint excursion function is a monotone function of the marginal exceedance probability, so the joint and marginal maps rank locations identically; they differ only in where the cutoff for "included" falls.
- Stated limitation: "One drawback with this parametric family is that it does not take the spatial dependency of the data into account directly." The sets considered are always top-k by marginal probability, so a spatially contiguous set that is not a top-k set is not in the family. A two-parameter smoothing family (Definition 3.4) is offered; in their 2D simulation it made the estimated sets "on average only 0.2% smaller", and the authors say "arguably not worth the extra computational effort" for that setting.
- Hyperparameter uncertainty is handled by three options for the non-Gaussian-in-theta posterior: EB (empirical Bayes, ignore it), QC (quantile correction), NI (numerical integration over INLA-style hyperparameter configurations). In the simulation (Example 3) NI was best, and the authors say the QC method performs well for large values of 1 - alpha.
- Non-Gaussian likelihoods: the paper assumes pi(x | y, theta) Gaussian (or a Gaussian approximation) and says handling other cases is "outside the scope of this article". (Our Poisson likelihood falls under this caveat for the Gaussian-integral route; the Monte Carlo route in the 2018 paper below does not require Gaussianity.)

** Spatial correlation
Modeled in full: the posterior covariance between locations enters the joint exceedance probability. The paper's own evidence on ignoring it is the comparison to the marginal set and Holm-Bonferroni bounds: in the Piemonte air pollution application (daily PM10 on 2006-01-30, level 50 micrograms per cubic meter, alpha = 0.1) the union of the positive and negative excursion sets "covers only a small part of the region, indicating that the uncertainty in the problem is large", and the authors stress the positive set "is small because the uncertainty is large in the problem, and not because the other regions certainly have concentrations below the level." No numerical false-positive-rate comparison between joint and marginal sets is reported in what I read. Calibration checks: Example 1 (1D, 50,000 Monte Carlo draws) shows 1 - alpha - phat(alpha) within about 0.01 (y axis in units of 1e-3, read off the axis); Example 3 (2D, 50 datasets, 60,000 draws each) shows averaged errors whose axis spans about -0.06 to 0.02 (read off the axis, not tabulated), with NI closest to zero.

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: the definition of a joint (simultaneous) exceedance set, the explanation of why marginal sets are anti-conservative for the "all locations in the set" event, the excursion function as a map of joint evidence, and the contour-uncertainty idea.
- Limits: (a) the sets are defined for exceedance of a level by a field (or by a difference field if one forms it), and the family restricts candidate sets to nested top-k-by-marginal-probability sets; we should not describe excursion sets as optimal spatial regions. (b) The paper's validation is for Gaussian likelihoods with hyperparameter handling options. (c) The probability statement is about the set as a whole: "all locations in the set exceed u with probability at least 1 - alpha", not that each location individually has that probability.

** What uncertainty it produces
Software description. Functions for excursion sets and contour credible regions, excursion functions, contour-map quality measures (P0, P1, P2), simultaneous confidence bands (=simconf=), and Gaussian integrals (=gaussint=), for Gaussian input, R-INLA output, or Monte Carlo samples. ** Monte Carlo functions (asked for explicitly)
- Paper (PDF p. 9 to 10): =excursions.mc= takes "a d × N matrix X containing N Monte Carlo simulations of the d dimensional process of interest" (p. 9). The paper says the function does "all computations purely based on the Monte Carlo samples that are provided" and "it does not use any of the computational methods based on sequential importance sampling for Gaussian integrals that the is the basis for the previous methods" (p. 9), is therefore more general because "X can be samples from any model, not necessarily a latent Gaussian model", and that "the only way of increasing the accuracy of the results is to increase the number of Monte Carlo samples that are provided to the function" (p. 10). (Quotes copied with the paper's typos.)
- Source of =excursions.mc= (v2.5.11). (1) Marginal exceedance probabilities rho are estimated as the row means of (X > u) over the draws (unless supplied). (2) Nodes are ordered by sorting rho in increasing order (function =excursions.permutation=), so the set always consists of the nodes with the largest marginal probabilities, nested as the set grows. (3) The excursion function value at position i of the ordering is the fraction of draws in which EVERY node from position i to the end of the ordering (i.e. the nodes with the largest marginal probabilities) exceeds u; this is computed by a reverse cumulative product of the indicator over the sorted nodes (function =mcint=). (4) The excursion set E is the set of nodes whose value exceeds 1 - alpha. The MC error field is a placeholder ("Estimate of MC error, not implemented yet"). Consequences (inference from the source): the joint probability is an empirical proportion with resolution 1/N for N draws, so values below 1/N appear as 0 and alpha near or below 1/N cannot be resolved; the construction relies on the same marginal-probability ordering as the Gaussian method; there is no Gaussianity assumption.
- Source of =simconf.mc=: pointwise bands are the marginal sample quantiles at alpha/2 and 1 - alpha/2. The simultaneous band uses the same marginal quantile construction with a common level rho, and rho is chosen by golden-section search on [0, alpha] (stopping when the bracket is narrower than 1e-4) so that the fraction of draws lying inside the band at ALL nodes is about 1 - alpha. The paper's definition (PDF p. 4-5) is a band {(s, y): q_rho(s) <= y <= q_{1-rho}(s)} with rho chosen so that P(q_rho(s) < X(s) < q_{1-rho}(s) for all s in Omega) = 1 - alpha.
- Monotonicity and ordering. Paper (PDF p. 5): to save computation time it is assumed that the excursion sets are nested (the set for alpha1 is contained in the set for alpha2 whenever alpha1 > alpha2), so F can be obtained by "first reordering the nodes and then computing a sequential integral", and "The reordering is in this case obtained by sorting the marginal probabilities P(xi > u) (for other options, see Bolin and Lindgren 2015)." So the parametric family is the marginal-probability family of Definition 3.2 in the 2015 paper, and nestedness is assumed rather than derived. A separate use of the word monotonicity (PDF p. 6): the continuous-domain interpolation of F between mesh nodes assumes monotonicity of the random field between the discrete computation locations (paraphrase), using log-linear interpolation; this is about the field between nodes, not about marginal probabilities.

** How uncertainty is used to compare locations, regions, transects, fields
Joint excursion sets and simultaneous bands, both joint, with multiplicity handled by calibrating a single set or band to joint probability 1 - alpha. Demonstrated on a Tokyo rainfall binomial time series (marginal versus simultaneous bands, Figure 3, no numbers) and Parana (Brazil) precipitation (Gamma likelihood; contour map with 1, 2 or 3 levels; P2 quality measure values 1, 0.4786 and 0.0048 respectively, PDF p. 16).

** Spatial correlation
Modeled through the joint distribution. The paper states "A problem with using pointwise confidence bands is that there is not joint interpretation, and one is therefore often of interested in computing simultaneous confidence bands." No type I error study is in this paper.

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: =excursions.mc= and =simconf.mc= are documented for arbitrary posterior draws, so applying them to draws of Poisson-model rate fields and of the pointwise-minimum signaling field is within what the package says it does. Simultaneous bands for transects can be cited to this paper.
- Limits: (a) accuracy is governed by the number of draws, with the 1/N resolution noted above (inference from source); report N and, ideally, a repeat with a different seed. (b) The excursion set from draws is a top-k set by marginal probability, and the joint event is "all nodes in the set exceed u", not an optimal region. (c) The band from =simconf.mc= is a widening of the marginal quantile band to a common level, so it is a band for the function as a whole at the nodes used. (d) Our posterior mixes over hyperparameters (draws from INLA), which =excursions.mc= takes as given.

** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
A negative binomial spatially varying coefficient model for gene m' expression regressed on gene m, X^m(s_k) given X^{m'}(s_k), with a spatial fused horseshoe prior on the spatially varying slope beta_1(s) over an MST (or k nearest neighbor or Delaunay graph), fit by MCMC (5,000 iterations, 2,500 burn-in in most analyses). The quantity is the slope surface beta_1(s) (local co-expression) and its tissue-wide average (global). A spatial random intercept beta_0(s) absorbs the autocorrelation of the response gene.

** How uncertainty is used
- Global test: based on the credible interval of the average slope; local test: "directly based on the credible intervals" of the location-level slopes. P-value-like metric: probability of direction, defined as the proportion of the posterior sharing the sign of the median, which "resembles a 2-sided frequentist P-value". Multiplicity: Benjamini-Hochberg across LR pairs via =p.adjust= for the global test. For the local, location-level test, I did not find a multiplicity correction across locations in what I read ("not stated in what I read"); the text says the local test is "directly based on the credible intervals", i.e. pointwise.
- Pointwise credible intervals, not joint bands or excursion sets.

** Spatial correlation and evidence on ignoring it (the numbers)
Setting. Locations: the 293 spots of a cutaneous melanoma ST (spatial transcriptomics) dataset (100 micrometer spots, 200 micrometer center-to-center). Observations simulated as negative binomial via a Gaussian copula with an exponential-kernel covariance H_{k1,k2} = exp(-L1 distance / l). Null: slope nu = 0 (the two genes independent but each spatially autocorrelated). Nominal level shown as a dotted line at 0.05. Number of simulation replicates: not stated in what I read.
- Design 1 (linear association, X^{m'} spatial, X^m generated from it; lengthscale l = 3.6, 7.2, 18). - Statements in the text: "all other methods suffer from inflated Type 1 errors", Pearson "while still inflated, performs better" than Moran's I or Lee's L approaches, and the inflation "becomes more pronounced as l increases"; Lee's L "exhibits the highest inflation in the majority of cases".
- Theory in the supplement (Section 2.3 and 2.4): Pearson correlation has a large variance when both variables are spatially autocorrelated, which "explains its inflated type 1 error in simulations"; for bivariate Moran's I under the null of independence the asymptotic mean tends to 0 but the variance can be large, "particularly when their spatial structure is aligned".
- The authors' caveat (response to reviewers): lengthscales "were selected to span regimes of increasing correlation range"; at l = 18 "most off-diagonal entries of the kernel covariance matrix approach 1"; they "did not estimate l from real data" and chose the values "to stress-test the method and cover worst-case scenarios". So the strongest inflation is in extreme-correlation regimes.
- Design choices that limit generality: the spots are the 293 locations of one dataset; "results remain unaffected" for other location sets (statement, no data shown, revision PDF p. 38).

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: independence-assuming association statistics are reported to have rejection rates far above 0.05 under a null of independent but spatially autocorrelated variables, with the inflation growing with the correlation range. This is the setting in our paper's naive comparator (tests that treat bins as independent), but SpaceBF's statistics (Pearson, Moran's I, Lee's L, SpatialDM, SpaGene) test gene-gene association, not differences between regions or fields. Cite as analogous evidence, not as a direct measurement of the Wilcoxon-on-bins comparison.
- Limits: (a) numbers exist only in figures, and the replicate count is not stated; (b) 293 spots, not 26,000 bins; (c) extreme lengthscales are acknowledged; (d) SpaceBF's own local test is pointwise and (in what I read) not corrected across locations, so it is a contrasting method (pointwise credible intervals) rather than a precedent for joint excursion inference; (e) the SpaceBF paper itself does not use excursion sets.

** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
No posterior or interval. Per location i it computes a score, the LRscore (ligand-receptor score) = L_i times R_i, where L_i is a kernel-weighted sum of ligand expression in the neighborhood and R_i the receptor expression at i (diffusion-dependent interactions: Gaussian kernel with default radius 200 micrometers; contact-dependent: Delaunay triangulation neighbors), followed by spatial smoothing with a Gaussian kernel within 200 micrometers. Uncertainty enters only as a permutation p-value per location per LR pair.

** How significance is assessed; independence; multiple testing
- Permutation (Methods): "We then performed spatial permutation tests ... We assumed that the locations of cells are independently distributed. By default, we set the permutation size to 100,000. Given n cells in the dataset, we permuted the whole dataset 100,000/n times to generate a dummy dataset." Each dummy dataset is processed by the standard pipeline (neighbors, LRscore, smoothing) to give a null distribution for each LR pair; "for each LR interaction, we calculated a P value by comparing the observed LRscore to the generated null distribution and performed spatial FDR correction" (reference 64 in the PMC list is Lun, Richard and Marioni, "Testing for differential abundance in mass cytometry data"; I did not read it, and the paper's text does not describe the correction in detail).
- The null as stated (Supplementary Note, PDF p. 6; same text in bioRxiv v1): "a one-sided p-value for the null hypothesis that the signal strength observed for a particular cell is no larger than expected based on the ligand and receptor expression levels within the tissue."
- Whether bins are treated as independent: the permutation shuffles locations of cells, and the authors state the independence assumption in the sentence quoted above. (Inference, not stated by the authors: because shuffling locations removes the spatial autocorrelation of each gene, the null is complete spatial randomness of each gene given its marginal distribution, so a spatially structured ligand and receptor that are not co-localized may also score above the null after smoothing; I did not test this.) The text of the statement "assumed that the locations of cells are independently distributed" is present in the Nature Genetics Methods; I did not find the same sentence in the bioRxiv v1 text.
- Calibration of the per-location permutation p-values under spatial autocorrelation is not reported in what I read. The only false-positive evidence is for the separate multi-sample differential test (negative binomial mixed model adapted from NEBULA, Wald chi-square p-values, Benjamini-Hochberg across interactions): median false-positive rate 0 in real Stereo-seq replicates and in scDesign3 simulations (Figure 5b, figure only).

** Use of the Stereo-seq mouse embryo data
Stereo-seq MOSTA whole-embryo sections, E9.5 to E16.5 (eight time points), data from CNGBdb; reprocessed to spliced and unspliced counts, "bin[ned] ... using the same bin size as the original analysis" (supplement PDF p. 29; the bin size is not stated in what I read). Results are shown for specific E12.5 slices (Dll1-Notch1 in slice E1S2; Fgf8-Fgfr1 in E1S1), the E12.5 to E16.5 brain region for contact versus diffusion counts (Figure 3e), and velocity on E10.5 to E13.5; it states 209 significant diffusion-dependent and 49 contact-dependent interactions for the slice summarized in the circos plots. E16.5 appears among the eight datasets used for run-time benchmarking (72 runs) and the time series in Figure 3e. Validation against PLA (proximity ligation assay) is on Visium HD sections and, qualitatively, E12.5 Stereo-seq (Extended Data Figs. 1 and 2, not read since they are figures).

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: CytoSignal is a published method on the same MOSTA Stereo-seq data that estimates a per-location ligand-receptor score as a product of (smoothed) ligand and receptor expression and flags locations by a permutation null; it gives a point-estimate-plus-p-value approach with no joint uncertainty, which is the "naive" style our uncertainty analysis contrasts with.
- Limits: it does not model between-bin correlation in its null beyond what the permutation destroys, states the independence assumption, and reports no calibration for the per-location p-values. Our statement about it should be limited to those verifiable points; we should not say that it "inflates false positives" without a test. Our signaling field uses a pointwise minimum of fitted rates, theirs a product of smoothed observed expression; the two are not the same quantity.

* smiDE, Vasconcelos et al. 2026

** Citation and what was read
Vasconcelos AG, McGuire D, Simon N, Danaher P, Shojaie A (2026). "Differential expression analysis for spatially correlated data using smiDE." Genome Biology 27: 21. doi:10.1186/s13059-025-03867-1. (DE = differential expression; ST = spatial transcriptomics.)

Read: full text from PMC12882255 (JATS XML; Background, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, Methods), cleaned: =/var/home/aoz/data/a-s-omics-lit/smide/PMC12882255.clean.txt=. Additional file 1 (18 pages: supplementary figures S1 to S21 captions and Tables S1 to S3): =smide/supp/13059_2025_3867_MOESM1_ESM.txt=. Limitation: in the PMC XML several in-line numbers are images (for example the type I error values for SPDE and for BYM2 in the main text, and the overlap-ratio cutoff); I could not read those. Figures were not inspected.

** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
Per-gene, per-covariate effect (log fold change) for cell-level ST data (CosMx, MERFISH), from regression with Poisson, negative binomial or Gaussian likelihood, with an optional spatial random effect: Gaussian process, cluster-level effects, SPDE with INLA (penalized-complexity priors on range and marginal standard deviation), or BYM2 (Besag-York-Mollie, adjacency-based). Frequentist fits give Wald p-values with Bonferroni; INLA fits give posterior means and credible intervals.

** How uncertainty is used to compare
Per-gene test of a covariate effect. Multiplicity across genes: "we form Bonferroni-adjusted equal-tailed credible intervals and declare DE if its credible interval excludes zero", described as "a conservative, family-wise multiplicity adjustment that is directly comparable to frequentist Bonferroni control". Pointwise (per-gene) intervals with a Bonferroni correction across genes; no joint excursion sets and no joint bands across space. Pseudo-p-values from the posterior probability of the sign are used for ranking only.

** Spatial correlation, and the evidence (numbers)
- Simulation (Methods, Simulations): log-Gaussian Cox process with a Gaussian-process random field (exponential covariance, range 1 in the units of the data), coordinates fixed at those of 5,878 macrophages from the CosMx NSCLC (non-small-cell lung cancer) data, testing stroma versus myeloid-enriched stroma; "100 replicates of 21 genes: 6 null genes ... and 15 non-null genes", so 600 null tests per parameter combination (my arithmetic). Type I error is the rejection rate among null genes; the nominal level is not stated in the text I read (the "near-nominal" values suggest 0.05).
- Non-spatial methods: "consistently exhibited severely inflated type I error, ranging from 0.86 (CSIDE) to 0.95 (DEseq2)" (Seurat Wilcoxon, NB GLMs, MAST, DESeq2 are all in this group). Cell-level GP: "near-nominal calibration (type I error 0.057)" at about 7 hours per gene (NB); Gaussian GP 0.067 at 20 minutes per gene. Cluster GP at K/n = 30 percent: 0.077 to 0.097. Independent-cluster models 0.38 to 0.45 at K/n about 5 percent and up to 0.80 as cluster size grows. SPDE via INLA: "near-nominal" (the number is an image in the PMC XML); Additional file 1 Table S1 gives type I error for SPDE-INLA with a dense mesh as 0.073 to 0.077 (negative binomial) and 0.105 to 0.128 (Gaussian) across prior settings, and BYM2 (K/n = 100 percent) 0.222 to 0.233 (negative binomial) and 0.158 to 0.168 (Gaussian). So, in these simulations, the INLA-SPDE approach is closer to nominal than naive methods but is not exactly at the nominal level.
- Real data. MERFISH whole brain, excitatory neurons in isocortex, 426 genes tested for association with microglia exposure: naive smiDE 178 DE genes at Bonferroni (262 with FDR); CSIDE 252; niche-DE 269 (NB) and 241 (Gaussian). After contamination filtering (56 of 178 removed, 31.5 percent), neighbor-expression adjustment (19 more, 10.7 percent), and spatial modeling (100 more, 56.2 percent), 3 remained with the SPDE dense mesh (negative binomial); "less than 2% of hits from naive models were fully explainable by factors other than the design variable" (as worded). Colon CosMx, B cells, 2,922 cells, local immune-cell count as covariate: naive 141 significant genes; semi-naive (gene filter and neighbor covariate) 114; spatially aware (GP-INLA) 9; the authors say 27 of the 141 "can be explained as resulting from errors in cell segmentation" and 105 "could be explained by spatial variability unrelated to the biological question". These are counts of genes lost, not verified false positives; the authors treat the lost genes as "potentially false discoveries".
- The authors' own limitation: spatial confounding. When the covariate "has strong spatial structure" (their examples: tissue niches or brain regions), a spatial random effect can "attenuate or mask the true covariate signal".

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: strong, specific numbers that independence-assuming tests (Wilcoxon, NB GLM, DESeq2, MAST) reject null genes at rates 0.86 to 0.95 in a simulation where nominal is presumably 0.05, and that adding a spatial random effect brings it near nominal. Also an example of INLA SPDE used in the same way as ours (random field plus likelihood) in a spatial-omics DE paper, with Bonferroni-adjusted credible intervals as the decision rule.
- Limits: (a) cell-level data with a CosMx coordinate set, not 50-bin Stereo-seq sums; (b) their simulated null is generated with one exponential-kernel GP of range 1 and fixed coordinates; (c) the main-text numbers for SPDE are not fully readable in what I have, and Table S1 shows INLA-SPDE type I error of about 0.07 to 0.13, so "near-nominal" is an approximate description; (d) the confounding limitation applies to any model that compares a spatially structured covariate (such as regions) to a spatial field, and may apply to our region-difference results, so we should state it; (e) credible intervals are pointwise per gene, not joint over space.

** Citation and what was read

** What uncertainty it produces, on what quantity
Per gene, per cell type k, per covariate effect alpha (log fold change), with standard errors from the Fisher information of the fitted Poisson-lognormal model: counts Y_ij ~ Poisson(N_i * lambda_ij), where N_i is the pixel's total UMI count (unique molecular identifier count), log(lambda_ij) = log(sum_k beta_ik mu_ikj) + gamma_j + epsilon_ij, with epsilon_ij a normal random effect for overdispersion, and log mu linear in covariates. So it also has total UMI as offset and Poisson counts, like our model, but the spatial structure enters only through covariates, not through a random field. Asymptotic normality gives standard errors and a two-sided z-test (Methods, Hypothesis testing). For multiple replicates: variance-weighted meta-analysis with a between-replicate variance.

** How uncertainty is used, multiplicity
Per-gene z-tests, then "q-values are calculated across all genes within a cell type to control the false discovery rate (FDR) using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure", FDR 0.01 (0.1 in the nonparametric case) with a fold-change cutoff of 1.5. For multi-region designs, p-values are scaled for the pairwise comparisons. Pointwise per-gene tests; there are no joint excursion sets or bands.

** Spatial correlation
Not modeled. The Supplementary Notes state that the likelihood uses "the assumption that measurements on separate pixels are independent" (PDF p. 3) and that, for the asymptotic theory, "we assume that these variables are generated i.i.d. for each pixel" (PDF p. 8). The main text, as far as I could find by keyword search and by reading Methods, contains no discussion of spatial correlation or autocorrelation between pixels (not stated in what I read). The epsilon term in the main text is an independent per-pixel overdispersion effect (normal with variance sigma squared). The simulation that supports "false positive rate was accurately controlled" builds each pixel from "randomly chos[en]" single cells ("we randomly chose a cell from each cell type, and sampled a predefined number of UMIs from each cell (total 1, 000)") in two regions; as described, it contains no between-pixel correlation mechanism (inference from the description), so it does not test calibration under spatial correlation. The replicate-level meta-analysis addresses variation across replicates, not within a slide. The authors note that multiple replicates "produce more robust DE estimates by reducing spurious discoveries of DE on single replicates."

Evidence from outside: smiDE (above) reports C-SIDE type I error 0.86 in their cell-level simulation with spatial correlation, and writes that C-SIDE "does not explicitly model spatial correlation"; that is smiDE's result, not C-SIDE's, and smiDE notes C-SIDE and niche-DE "are more commonly applied to spot-based ST, where correlation is typically weaker".

** What it means for our paper
- Supports: a widely cited method that tests spatial DE across regions or covariates with standard errors that assume independent pixels (per its own supplementary statement), then BH across genes. It is a direct instance of the "treat bins as independent" practice for region comparisons, with Poisson counts and total UMI offset like ours.
- Limits: it was designed for cell type mixtures at the spot or pixel level, tests one gene at a time for a covariate effect, and the authors do not claim calibration under spatial correlation. Its independence assumption is stated in the supplement, not the main text. We should say "assumes independence across pixels in its likelihood" and attribute the inflation evidence to smiDE, not to C-SIDE. Our regional-average comparison is not the same estimand (an average of a fitted rate field within a region rather than a regression coefficient with cell-type deconvolution).
