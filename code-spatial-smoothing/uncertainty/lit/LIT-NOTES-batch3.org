* SpatialDM
** Citation and what was read
Li Z, Wang T, Liu P, Huang Y. SpatialDM for rapid identification of spatially co-expressed ligand-receptor and revealing cell-cell communication patterns. Nature Communications 2023;14:3995. doi:10.1038/s41467-023-39608-w (PMC10325966).
- Read in full: the open-access main text (Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, all Methods) as PMC XML converted to text: lit dir/spatialdm/spatialdm.txt (343 lines, about 8,900 words; the reference list was not read).
- Read in full: Supplementary Information PDF (Supplementary Note 1, the derivation of the analytical null, plus Supplementary Figure legends; the figure panels themselves were not inspected): lit dir/spatialdm/supp1.txt (503 lines).
- Downloaded but only the first 30 lines viewed: Nature Portfolio reporting summary (lit dir/spatialdm/f2.txt). Not read: Supplementary Datasets 1-8, Peer Review File, Source Data (the download URLs for those returned XML error pages, not files).
** What significance is reported, how computed
- Quantity tested: the global bivariate Moran's R for each candidate LR pair, with x_i the normalized log-transformed ligand expression at spot i and y_j the receptor expression at spot j, weighted by a radial-basis-function kernel w_ij = (n/W) exp(-d_ij^2 / (2 l^2)) (d_ij = distance between spots i and j, l = length scale in coordinate units, n = number of spots, W = sum of unnormalized weights). Null: ligand and receptor "spatially independent".
- Two nulls (Methods, "Hypothesis testing with global Moran's R"): (1) permutation (shuffling, e.g. 1000 times); (2) an analytical normal approximation (z-score) from the first and second moments of R. Wording differs between sections: the Results say "random shuffling of binding partners for each pair", the Methods say "shuffling wij". I did not see the code.
- What the analytical null assumes (Supplementary Note 1, section 1.2): "X and Y are normally distributed: X ~ N(0, sigma_1^2 I), Y ~ N(0, sigma_2^2 I)" and "X and Y are is spatially randomly distributed" (typo in original). I.e., the derivation takes each gene's values as independent across spots. A pair in which both genes are individually spatially structured but unrelated is not addressed in the text I read. For local R_i, the Note states each x_i, x_j pair is independent.
- Spatial autocorrelation within a gene: one related feature is described. A "fine tune" term adds ligand/receptor univariate Moran's I to R, with weight 0.17 for the ligand in simulations and 0 for all experimental datasets (Methods, "Fine tune with auto-correlation weights"; Supplementary Figure 2f compares AUROC with and without it). So the real-data p-values do not use it.
- Local test: local bivariate R_i = sender part + receiver part, with analytical variance; spots flagged at per-spot p < 0.1, then restricted by a quadrant rule (higher-than-average sender or receiver signal). The Supplementary Figure 1 legend says "the selected number of spots refers to only spots with the uncorrected p < 0.1" and "All statistical results are based on one-sided tests without adjustment" (SVZ example). The spacia paper's benchmark call to SpatialDM lists "sdm.sig_spots with options method=permutation and fdr=False", which suggests the software has an FDR option for spots (second-hand, from the spacia methods text; I did not read the SpatialDM code).
- Calibration evidence: simulations adapted from SVCA; with 0% variance explained by interaction the p-values are "well calibrated to a uniform distribution"; in the pooled positive/negative set at p < 0.05 the paper reports power 74.5% and a false positive rate of 8.2% (z-score mode). In real melanoma data with a shuffled LR list (663 pairs), 90 pairs had permutation p < 0.05 (about 13.6%, my arithmetic: 90/663); the authors describe this as good false-positive control relative to the other methods.
** Multiplicity
Global: FDR < 0.1 across the LR pairs (melanoma 1180 pairs, 103 significant). The specific FDR procedure is not named in the text I read (a search for "Benjamini" returned nothing). Local spots: p < 0.1, uncorrected as described above. No correction across the family of spots within a pair is described in the text I read.
** Comparisons between conditions
Differential analysis (Methods, "Differential analyses"): global z-scores of 1,486 pairs from 6 colon slices (2 adult, 4 fetal); for each pair a linear model with versus without a condition term, likelihood ratio test (chi-square). Missing ligand or receptor sets z to 0. Unit of replication is the slice, not the spot. Text reports 146 pairs higher in adult and 97 in fetal at FDR < 0.1; the Figure 4a legend says 135 adult-specific and 98 fetal-specific (a discrepancy within the paper that I did not resolve). The paper's Discussion notes that with few replicates this is hard and "a Bayesian treatment for jointly analysing all pairs may mitigate this issue". No test comparing regions within a slice, and no uncertainty on the fields.
** Verdict on the manuscript's current claim
Manuscript claim: "tests ligand-receptor pairs for spatial co-expression using a bivariate extension of Moran's I, both globally ... and locally, identifying the spots that drive the association, with significance from an analytically derived null distribution."
Verdict: supported, with two refinements. (a) The analytical (z-score) null is one of two options; the paper also offers permutation, and it uses permutation for small datasets (melanoma, SVZ) and the analytical null for the larger intestine data. (b) The analytical null is derived under independence of spots within each gene (Supplementary Note 1). Local "hits" are called at per-spot p < 0.1, apparently uncorrected. The authors call the statistic "Moran's R" and the spots "local interaction hits".
** Relevance to our paper; what we can cite; limits
- Citable: SpatialDM tests spatial co-expression of ligand and receptor point estimates (log-normalized expression), reports a p-value per pair and a per-spot p-value map, and does not report uncertainty on the underlying expression fields. Its analytical null assumes spatially unstructured genes (Supplementary Note 1). Local hits at uncorrected p < 0.1 per spot is a concrete example of a map built from many individually thresholded locations, the situation excursion sets are meant to address (we should say "as described" and not claim it is wrong).
- Limit on our claims: SpatialDM does cover between-condition comparison (per pair, per slice, with replicates), so "no existing method compares signaling between conditions" must not be written. What we can say is that the comparison is at the slice level.
- Also noted: SpatialDM applies to counts transformed by normalization and log, not to a count model with an offset.

* COMMOT
** Citation and what was read
Cang Z, Zhao Y, Almet AA, Stabell A, Ramos R, Plikus MV, Atwood SX, Nie Q. Screening cell-cell communication in spatial transcriptomics via collective optimal transport. Nature Methods 2023;20(2) (volume and issue per Crossref in lit dir/batch3.bib; page numbers not recorded). doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01728-4 (DOI verified: NCBI maps it to PMC9911355, the paper in question).
- Read: open-access main text including Methods (author manuscript in PMC) as XML converted to text: lit dir/commot/commot.txt (374 lines, about 9,700 words). Equations were dropped or garbled by the conversion; references not read.
- Not read: the Supplementary Note (algorithm derivations, comparisons with CellChat, Giotto, CellPhoneDB), Supplementary Figures, Extended Data figures other than captions. Statements about significance below are limited to the main text and Methods.
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- Core output: for each LR pair and each pair of spots, a "collective optimal transport" coupling quantifying the ligand contributed by one spot to the ligand-receptor complex in another, subject to a spatial range limit and competition between ligand and receptor species. This is a deterministic optimization (Sinkhorn iterations with entropic regularization); I found no uncertainty interval or p-value on spot-level signaling in the text read.
- Cluster level (Methods, "Cluster-level CCC"): the spot-by-spot matrix is aggregated by cluster labels, and "the significance (P value) of the cluster-level CCC is determined by performing n independent permutations of the cluster labels". The authors note that this "may neglect communications between different clusters" and offer an option to permute the locations of all spots or the spots within each cluster and recompute the spot-level matrices. So the permutation null treats spots as exchangeable within the permuted unit. The correction for multiple pairs or clusters is not stated in the text I read. The Methods also describe Giotto analysis "with the adjust_method set to fdr" for the comparison method.
- Downstream (Results and Methods): the received signal r_i = sum_j S_ji at each spot is used as a cofactor in tradeSeq (pseudotime-style DE) to find "differentially expressed CCC genes", then a random forest ranks the target genes. Spots enter as observations; dependence among spots is not discussed in the text I read.
- Validation is correlational: median Spearman correlations between received signal and known downstream gene expression of 0.237, 0.180, 0.230 in three datasets (Results, Robust identification of CCC direction). Robustness checks use subsampling (Drosophila embryo).
- The Discussion acknowledges that "false positives in our inferred CCC are inherently possible" because mRNA is not protein.
** Comparisons
Between clusters (cluster-level CCC networks, Jaccard distance between networks for robustness); between spatial regions via signaling-direction vector fields (cosine distance for agreement). No credible-difference calculation between regions.
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: COMMOT scores are deterministic summaries of point-estimated expression, with permutation significance only at the cluster level and (in the text read) no uncertainty on spot-level or region-level signaling.
- COMMOT states that "current methods examine CCC locally and on cell pairs independently" and positions collective transport as the remedy for ignoring competition; this is about biophysical competition, not statistical dependence.
- Limit: the Supplementary Note might contain further uncertainty analysis; I could not read it.
** Quotes
- "The significance (P value) of the cluster-level CCC is determined by performing n independent permutations of the cluster labels and computing the percentile of the original signaling strength in the signaling strengths resulting from these label permutations." (Methods, Cluster-level CCC; src=commot/commot.txt)
- "Permuting cluster labels after computing the spot-level CCC matrices may neglect communications between different clusters." (Methods, Cluster-level CCC; src=commot/commot.txt)
- "current methods examine CCC locally and on cell pairs independently" (Main text, Introduction; src=commot/commot.txt)
- "We acknowledge that false positives in our inferred CCC are inherently possible" (Discussion; src=commot/commot.txt)

* BATCOM
** Citation and what was read
Wu D, Gaskins JT, Sekula M, Datta S. Inferring Cell-Cell Communications from Spatially Resolved Transcriptomics Data Using a Bayesian Tweedie Model. Genes 2023;14(7):1368. doi:10.3390/genes14071368 (PMC10379215, open access).
- Read in full: Abstract, Introduction, Methods (2.1 to 2.4), Results (3.1 simulation headings and the 3.2 case study), Discussion, from lit dir/batcom/batcom.txt (about 8,600 words). The simulation subsections 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 and the appendices (gradient formulas, numerical tables) were not read line by line; I relied on the Discussion's statements about them. Tables are not in the text file.
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- Model: for LR pair k, spot-to-spot score C_ij^k = L_i^k x R_j^k (ligand at sender spot i times receptor at receiver spot j), modeled by a Tweedie (compound Poisson-Gamma) GLM with log link: mean depends on sum over cell-type pairs of beta_{g1,g2} M_{i,g1} M_{j,g2} exp(-rho D_ij) plus random effects nu_i^L and nu_j^R (M = cell-type proportions from deconvolution, D = distance, rho = fixed decay parameter, 0.5 in the case study). N^2 spot pairs enter as observations (optionally restricted by distance).
- Uncertainty: posterior samples of beta by Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (10,000 retained). Inference is then made frequentist-style: a Wald statistic W = (posterior mean)^2 / (posterior variance) is compared to chi-square(1), giving a "pseudo-p-value", adjusted by the false discovery rate (FDR) procedure, declared significant at FDR-adjusted p < 0.05 (Methods 2.4.2). The posterior is therefore reduced to a mean and variance and used for per-coefficient tests; no joint region or band is constructed.
- Dependence: the authors state that the C_ij^k "are not independent of each other as they partially come from the same spot" and add sender and receiver spot random effects. In the main Bayesian step "we treat the estimated nu^L and nu^R as fixed parameters ... to reduce the computational burden", so uncertainty in those effects is not propagated. Spatial correlation among neighboring spots beyond this shared-sender/receiver structure is not modeled in the text I read; the distance enters only through the mean decay.
- Multiplicity: FDR across all cell-type interactions (Methods 2.4.2). The authors also note that SpaTalk "does not correct the p-values for multiple comparisons" (Results 3.2).
** Comparisons
Coefficients are interpreted as communication strength between cell-type pairs (positive or negative direction). Comparison with Giotto, COMMOT and SpaTalk is by overlap of significant calls (UpSet plot); the authors say these differ greatly and that with no ground truth "it is difficult to determine the most appropriate method at the biological level". No comparison of regions of a field.
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: BATCOM is a Bayesian model with a posterior for cell-type-pair communication coefficients, i.e. the posterior is used for coefficient-level inference, not for fields. It collapses the posterior to a Wald-type pseudo-p-value (Methods 2.4.2).
- Limit: it shows that posterior-based uncertainty exists in the CCC literature, so we should not write that CCC methods are uniformly non-Bayesian. Its spatial dependence is minimal (shared-spot random effects fixed at estimates).
** Quotes
- "are not independent of each other as they partially come from the same spot" (Methods 2.2; src=batcom/batcom.txt)
- "as fixed parameters in the main Bayesian framework to reduce the computational burden" (Methods 2.4.1; src=batcom/batcom.txt)
- "The pseudo-p-values across all interactions of cell types will be adjusted for multiple hypothesis testing using the false discovery rate (FDR) correction" (Methods 2.4.2; src=batcom/batcom.txt)
- "SpaTalk does not correct the p-values for multiple comparisons" (Results 3.2; src=batcom/batcom.txt)

* spacia
** Citation and what was read
Zhu J, Wang Y, Chang WY, et al. Mapping Cellular Interactions from Spatially Resolved Transcriptomics Data. bioRxiv 2023, doi:10.1101/2023.09.18.558298 (PMC10541142). Published as Nature Methods 2024;21 (Crossref: doi:10.1038/s41592-024-02408-1, issued 2024-09-03; title "Mapping cellular interactions from spatially resolved transcriptomics data", abstract names a "multiple-instance learning framework", versus "Bayesian multi-instance learning" in the preprint).
- Read: the bioRxiv preprint main text and Methods (PMC XML converted): lit dir/spacia/spacia.txt (about 12,500 words). Not read: Supplementary Files 1 and 2 (technical details and additional analyses), Supplementary Table 1, and supplementary figures.
- Published Nature Methods version: only the Abstract and figure list were accessible (the page said "This is a preview of subscription content"; a PDF request returned an HTML page). Saved text of that page: lit dir/spacia/spacia-nm.txt (abstract and references only). All statistical statements below are from the preprint and may differ in the published version.
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- Model: a Bayesian multi-instance learning model. Each receiver cell is a "bag" of candidate sender cells (instances). Tier 1 decides, from spatial closeness, which senders truly act on the receiver (indicator delta, distance parameter b); tier 2 estimates weights beta from sender genes to receiver genes for senders with delta = 1. Intended for single-cell-resolution data (MERSCOPE, CosMx, Xenium); the authors say spacia "only considers the types of cell-cell communications that require the interacting cells to be closely localized".
- Uncertainty: MCMC (default 50,000 iterations) gives a posterior for b and beta; the preprint says "their confidence intervals are visualized ... according to the sampling from the MCMC iterations, from which we can also calculate P values". How a P value is computed from the samples is not stated in the text I read. Reported simulation P values: below 1E-4 for b, 0.008 to 3.4E-5 for truly interacting gene weights, 0.09 to 0.77 for non-interacting.
- Filtering rule used in real data (Methods, Statistics & reproducibility): interactions kept if b < 0, b P-value < 1x10E-30 and beta P-value < 0.01. This is a fixed threshold rule; no multiplicity correction across gene pairs or cell types is described in the text I read.
- Independence: the model is built around many-senders-to-one-receiver, which is a dependence structure across instances within a bag; whether receivers are treated as independent bags across the tissue is not discussed in the text I read.
** Comparisons
Compared with CellPhoneDB, CellChat, SpatialDM, COMMOT, SpaTalk by specificity (fraction of calls shared across sender cell types: spacia: 92.7% of calls unique to one or two sender cell types; the share of calls found in three or more sender types is reported as 52.2% for SpatialDM and 100% for COMMOT, per the authors' Figure 2d). That is the authors' benchmark on one prostate cancer dataset after downsampling the competitors, so it is their assessment, not independently verified. No region or field comparison.
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: spacia reports posterior-sample-based intervals and P values for its parameters, again at the parameter level, not for spatial fields.
- It targets single-cell-resolution data and gene-to-gene effects, so it is a different problem from ligand-receptor interaction fields on a binned section; keep the positioning short.
** Quotes
- "their confidence intervals are visualized in Fig. 1ef, according to the sampling from the MCMC iterations, from which we can also calculate P values" (Results, Multi-Instance Learning; src=spacia/spacia.txt)
- "Unless otherwise stated, the spacia results were filtered to keep those interactions satisfying b < 0, b Pval<1" (Methods, Statistics & reproducibility; src=spacia/spacia.txt)
- "spacia only considers the types of cell-cell communications that require the interacting cells to be closely localized" (Results; src=spacia/spacia.txt)

* SpatialDE
** Citation and what was read
Svensson V, Teichmann SA, Stegle O. SpatialDE: identification of spatially variable genes. Nature Methods 2018;15(5). doi:10.1038/nmeth.4636 (PMC6350895, author manuscript).
- Read in full: main text, Online Methods, Data sets, Expression count normalisation (about 3,700 words): lit dir/spatialde/spatialde.txt. Not read: Supplementary Note 1 (derivations, calibration analyses) and Supplementary Figures/Tables (the main text refers to calibration on randomized data, "Supp. Fig. 9", and simulations, "Supp. Fig. 10").
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- Model per gene: y ~ N(mu 1, sigma_s^2 (Sigma + delta I)), Sigma_ij = exp(-|x_i - x_j|^2 / (2 l^2)) (squared exponential kernel, l = length scale), delta I = independent non-spatial noise; fraction of spatial variance FSV = 1/(1 + delta). Maximum marginal likelihood fit, l by grid search.
- Test: likelihood of the fitted model versus a model with no spatial covariance, N(mu 1, sigma^2 I); p-value "estimated analytically based on the chi-square distribution transformation with one degree of freedom"; the Q-value method adjusts for multiple testing (FDR, with FDR < 0.05 used for calls).
- Data treatment: counts are Anscombe-transformed (variance stabilizing for negative binomial) and the log total count is regressed out before fitting, because "The SpatialDE model is based on the assumption of normally distributed residual noise and independent observations across cells" (Methods, Expression count normalisation). Calibration is checked on randomized data and negative-control probes (Results; the supplement is not read).
- Spatial autocorrelation: modeled by the kernel; the null is "no spatial covariance". Uncertainty on the fitted function is not reported in the text read (the output is FSV, length scale, p- and q-values; model selection among covariance functions by BIC).
** Comparisons
Gene-level only. The automatic expression histology module clusters spatially variable genes into patterns with posterior expectations; it does not test differences between regions. The paper contrasts with ANOVA across discrete groups, which "can only detect variations that are captured by differences between discrete groups".
** Verdict on the manuscript's current claim
Manuscript claim: "decomposing each gene's expression variance into a spatial component parameterized by a covariance kernel and a non-spatial noise component, then testing the spatial component by a likelihood ratio test."
Verdict: supported. Refinements available if wanted: the kernel is squared exponential (with linear and periodic alternatives compared by BIC), the fit is Gaussian on Anscombe-transformed, depth-regressed counts, the likelihood ratio statistic is referred to a chi-square with one degree of freedom, and multiplicity is handled by Q-values.
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: SpatialDE is the standard Gaussian-process test of whether a gene has any spatial variation; it answers "is there spatial structure" and not "where do two regions differ credibly".
- A contrast with our model: Gaussian likelihood on transformed counts versus our Poisson with offset; SpatialDE regresses out log total count, we use the total-UMI offset.
** Quotes
- "for each gene, SpatialDE decomposes expression variability into spatial and non-spatial components" (Introduction; src=spatialde/spatialde.txt)
- "we use the Q-value method" (Methods, Statistical significance; src=spatialde/spatialde.txt)
- "The SpatialDE model is based on the assumption of normally distributed residual noise and independent observations across cells." (Methods, Expression count normalisation; src=spatialde/spatialde.txt)

* SVCA (spatial variance component analysis)
** Citation and what was read
Arnol D, Schapiro D, Bodenmiller B, Saez-Rodriguez J, Stegle O. Modeling Cell-Cell Interactions from Spatial Molecular Data with Spatial Variance Component Analysis. Cell Reports 2019;29(1). doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2019.08.077 (PMC6899515).
- Read: Summary, Introduction, Results, Discussion, and the STAR Methods sections on the model, covariance terms, parameter inference, variance components, simulations, out-of-sample prediction, significance of variance components and p-value calibration (lit dir/svca/svca.txt, about 10,000 words). Not read in detail: the sub-sections comparing to HistoCAT, Battich 2015 and Goltsev 2018, the data processing details, and all supplementary figures and tables (Figures S1-S4, Table S1).
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- Model per molecule: Y ~ N(0, K_int + K_env + K_c-c + sigma_eps^2 I), with K_int = sigma_int^2 X X^T (linear kernel on the expression of all other genes, the "cell state"), K_env = sigma_E^2 exp(-d_ij^2 / (2 l^2)) (squared exponential on cell positions), K_c-c = sigma_c-c^2 Z X X^T Z^T with Z_ij = exp(-d_ij^2 / (2 l^2)), Z_ii = 0 (neighborhood composition), and independent Gaussian noise. Fitted by maximum likelihood; variance fractions via Gower factors.
- Test: the cell-cell interaction component is tested by a log likelihood ratio against a reduced model without it; the statistic is referred to a chi-square distribution whose parameter is fitted to an empirical null of 100 simulated likelihood ratios per test ("we calibrate this chi-square distribution by fitting its parameter to an empirical null distribution of LLRs obtained from simulations"). Benjamini-Hochberg across tests and a 1% FDR threshold; counted per protein as the number of images in which the component is significant. Calibration is reported as conservative in simulations.
- Which "interaction": the component measures dependence of a molecule's level on the expression composition of its spatial neighborhood, not on a specific ligand-receptor pair; the authors say it cannot tell co-occurrence of cell types from specific molecular interactions.
- Gaussian residuals are assumed; the Discussion lists a negative-binomial likelihood as future work.
** Comparisons
Images are compared by PCA of the "spatial variance signatures" (variance fractions per molecule), with a permutation-based separation test of tumor grades 1 and 3 (p = 3.8E-3 stated) and Benjamini-Hochberg enrichment of gene sets. No regional comparison within an image.
** Verdict on the manuscript's current claim
Manuscript claim: "fits an additive Gaussian-process random-effect model that decomposes the variance of each gene or protein into intrinsic, environmental, cell-cell interaction, and noise components, and tests whether the interaction component is nonzero ... developed for imaging-based data."
Verdict: supported. Notes: the paper's components are named exactly intrinsic, environmental, cell-cell interaction, noise; the interaction p-value comes from a likelihood ratio test with an empirically calibrated chi-square; applications are imaging mass cytometry (protein) and seqFISH (RNA); the paper says spatial transcriptomics "do not offer single-cell resolution". "Gaussian-process random-effect" is accurate (the paper calls it a Gaussian process / random effect model).
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: a Gaussian-process variance-decomposition test for neighborhood effects; per-molecule tests; multiplicity by Benjamini-Hochberg.
- The noise term is independent; residual spatial dependence is absorbed by the environmental term.
** Quotes
- "SVCA builds upon the random effect framework to model gene expression variation of individual genes as a function of additive components of intrinsic cell state effects" (Results; src=svca/svca.txt)

* DESpace and DESpace2
** Citation and what was read
- DESpace: Cai P, Robinson MD, Tiberi S. DESpace: spatially variable gene detection via differential expression testing of spatial clusters. Bioinformatics 2024;40(2):btae027. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae027 (PMC10868334).
- DESpace2: Cai P, Robinson MD, Tiberi S. DESpace2: detection of differential spatial patterns in spatial omics data. Bioinformatics 2026;42(Supplement_2):btag450. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btag450 (PMC13501297, open access, found by searching PMC; the preprint is bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.30.662268).
- Read: DESpace main text including Methods and Results (lit dir/despace/despace.txt, about 6,400 words) and DESpace2 main text including Methods (lit dir/despace/despace2.txt, about 5,800 words). Not read for either: the Supplementary material (e.g., DESpace2 Supplementary Sections S1.1 and S1.2, Tables S5-S7, DESpace Supplementary Figures); results subsections on simulations were skimmed through the Discussion and selected paragraphs, not line by line.
** What significance or uncertainty is reported
- DESpace (v1): a negative binomial model per gene on spot-level counts, log(mu_gi) = log(M_i) + beta_gc, with M_i the TMM-normalized library size of spot i and beta_gc the coefficient of spatial cluster c (clusters supplied from manual annotation or from BayesSpace, StLearn, etc.). Spatially variable gene (SVG) test: likelihood ratio test of equal beta across clusters, via edgeR; also a test per cluster to find "the main cluster affected". The model as written treats the spot counts as independent negative binomial draws given their cluster; the text I read does not discuss spatial correlation among spots in a cluster. Multiple testing: results are called at "1% FDR" and FDR is evaluated in simulations; the adjustment procedure is not named in the text I read. With replicates, a sample term gamma_gj is added.
- Circularity: the authors state they "are aware of the circularity (or double dipping) issue" (clusters are computed from the same data) and argue empirically it does not impair benchmarks.
- DESpace2: pseudobulk counts per sample and spatial domain, negative binomial GLM with domain, condition and domain-by-condition interaction terms; global test of all interaction coefficients, quasi-likelihood F-test by default (accounts for uncertainty in dispersion estimation) or likelihood ratio test; an individual-domain test relabels domain d versus the rest; "Benjamini-Hochberg Pvalue correction ... is applied to account for multiple testing".
- Statement on independence (DESpace2, Overview): marker-gene methods applied spot by spot "treat each spot as an independent replicate, neglecting the dependency between spots from the same sample, which makes them prone to inflated false positive rates". In their real-data example (an axolotl dataset, ARTISTA, comparing 2 and 20 DPI; DPI is not expanded in the text I read), spot-level FindMarkers/findMarkers called over 7,000 of 13,890 genes while DESpace2 and spatialLIBD called tens to a few hundred, "consistent with the inflation of false positives observed in simulation studies". The authors do not claim to have verified the cause beyond their simulations (Table S5, not read).
** Comparisons
Between spatial clusters (v1) and between conditions within or across domains (v2); the unit of replication in v2 is the sample. Regions are pre-specified or estimated clusters, the same labels used to define them, so inference is conditional on the clustering.
** Relevance to our paper
- Closest published statement of our reason for not trusting spot-level Wilcoxon tests: DESpace2 explicitly says treating spots as independent replicates inflates false positives (Overview) and uses pseudobulk by sample instead. Citable as "a position also taken in the spatial DE literature".
- DESpace v1 is a count model (negative binomial with library-size offset) at the spot level with cluster covariates; it compares regions but treats spots within a region as conditionally independent in the model as written.
- Limit: DESpace2's remedy is replication across samples. Our data are one section; our approach (posterior correlation between bins) addresses within-section dependence. Do not claim DESpace2 addresses single-section dependence; it does not.

* GASTON
** Citation and what was read
Chitra U, Arnold BJ, Sarkar H, Sanno K, Ma C, Lopez-Darwin S, Raphael BJ (author list per Crossref). Mapping the topography of spatial gene expression with interpretable deep learning. Nature Methods 2025;22(2). doi:10.1038/s41592-024-02503-3 (PMC12370209).
- Read: Abstract, Introduction, Results (Overview, cerebellum, continuous and discontinuous variation, tumor microenvironment, sparse Stereo-seq: olfactory bulb and mouse embryo), Discussion, Methods (long-range correlations and pooling, training and implementation, quantifying spatial variation, marker gene analysis, spatial coherence score, comparison methods); lit dir/gaston/gaston.txt (about 14,300 words). The earlier modeling subsections of the Methods (conservative vector fields, maximum likelihood estimation) were not read line by line. A keyword search over the entire text for uncertainty, confidence interval, bootstrap, credible, multiple testing, Bonferroni, Benjamini, FDR returned no hits. Not read: Supplementary Sections S1-S9, Supplementary Tables, Extended Data (captions only).
** Uncertainty and significance
- Model: each gene's expression is a piecewise linear function of a learned scalar "isodepth" d(x,y), with domains given by contours of d. Counts a_ig are modeled Poisson with mean U_i exp(f_g(x_i,y_i)) (U_i = total UMI at spot i), the same family as ours (Poisson with a total-UMI offset), but with a deterministic, parametric piecewise linear mean rather than a random field.
- Fitting: isodepth and breakpoints are first estimated on the top generalized linear model principal components under a Gaussian error model (30 random initializations, maximum likelihood), then fixed, and per-gene per-domain Poisson regressions are fit. Uncertainty in the isodepth coordinate or in the domain boundaries is not described in the text I read; no resampling or posterior is mentioned.
- Slope test: for each gene g and domain p, a likelihood ratio test of slope = 0, with "2 x LLR follows a chi-square distribution" (Wilks); the slope is set to zero if p < 0.1. Counts are treated as independent Poisson given the fitted function; the authors emphasize that their model captures "long-range" correlations because all locations with equal isodepth share a value. Multiplicity across the 2,000 to 5,000 genes and domains is not described for this step in the text read. Effect-size thresholds (tenth percentile of estimated slopes or discontinuity magnitudes per domain) then flag "intradomain variation" and "discontinuity". The text reports 15 to 40% of genes per domain with nonconstant slope at p < 0.05.
- Evaluation is against marker lists (AUPRC), not against calibration of uncertainty. Enrichment p-values in the Extended Data use multiple-comparison adjustment (Enrichr). The comparison methods (SpatialDE, SPARK-X, HotSpot, trendsceek, SpaGCN, C-SIDE) are ranked by their p-values.
** Comparisons along an axis
The isodepth is effectively a 1-D transect coordinate; comparisons are of fitted expression-versus-isodepth curves (slopes within domains, jumps at boundaries), summarized by effect-size thresholds and ranking, with domain-level likelihood ratio tests. There is no simultaneous band for h_g(d) and no test of a difference between two positions along the axis in the text I read.
** Relevance to our paper
- Closest analog to our transects: expression along a learned 1-D coordinate, Poisson with total-UMI offset. GASTON reports point estimates plus per-slope p-values that condition on a fixed isodepth and treat counts as independent given the fit. Citable as: "GASTON and Belayer fit expression along a one-dimensional coordinate and report point estimates and slope tests; uncertainty in the coordinate or in the whole curve is not reported in the text I read".
- GASTON analyzes a Stereo-seq mouse embryo at E9.5 (10 domains; 128 genes with continuous variation in the heart domain), which is a dataset of the same platform and species as ours; it does not address ligand-receptor fields.
- Limit: GASTON is a different aim (domain and gradient discovery); we must not suggest it was meant to give uncertainty bands.

- "which holds asymptotically by Wilks’ theorem" (Methods, Training and implementation; src=gaston/gaston.txt)

* Belayer
** Citation and what was read
Ma C, Chitra U, Zhang S, Raphael BJ. Belayer: Modeling discrete and continuous spatial variation in gene expression from spatially resolved transcriptomics. Cell Systems 2022;13(10). doi:10.1016/j.cels.2022.09.002 (PMC9814896).
- Read: Summary, Belayer Algorithm, Identifying spatially varying genes, Discussion, and STAR Methods passages on the Poisson model, binning, quantification and comparison methods and the Gaussian process relationship (lit dir/belayer/belayer.txt, about 15,900 words). The result sections on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex layers, mouse skin and somatosensory cortex, and the dynamic programming derivations, were not read line by line; keyword search (significance, hypothesis, permutation, p-value, uncertainty, confidence interval, bootstrap, credible, FDR, Bonferroni, Benjamini) found only the ranking-by-p-value statements for other methods and the LRT used as a ranking. Not read: Supplemental Figures and Tables.
** Uncertainty and significance
- Model: each gene is a piecewise linear function of relative depth within each layer, with discontinuities at layer boundaries; layers and conformal maps are estimated by maximum likelihood with dynamic programming. Counts: "we model UMI counts ... with a Poisson distribution" with mean C_i exp(f_g), C_i the total UMI at spot i.
- No standard errors, intervals, tests or multiplicity control are applied to the fitted layers or slopes in the text I read. Genes are ranked by the largest layer-specific slope (and by a likelihood ratio versus a piecewise constant model, used as a ranking) and evaluated by AUPRC against marker lists (e.g., 0.032 for Belayer versus 0.017 for SpatialDE ranking, 0.029 for SPARK and HotSpot, in the DLPFC slice 151508); the authors say all methods have low AUPRC.
- The Discussion lists as future work: "it would helpful to further minimize overfitting by incorporating regularization and rigorous statistical testing into our algorithm; e.g., using the Chow test".
- The Methods note that a piecewise linear function can be written as a Gaussian process with a linear covariance (a MAP version), but the posterior is not used for inference in the text read.
** Comparisons along an axis
Relative depth within layers is the 1-D axis; comparison across layers is by fitted slopes and jumps, ranked.
** Relevance to our paper
- Citable: Belayer is a count model (Poisson with a total-UMI offset) along a 1-D coordinate; the authors state that rigorous statistical testing is not included in the method. Supports the sentence that a transect-type analysis along a coordinate with simultaneous uncertainty is not what these methods provide.
- Limit: Belayer targets layered tissues (cortex, skin), where a layer axis exists; an embryo section has no single layer axis, so the comparison is analogy only.

* Cross-paper synthesis (statements we could use, and what limits them)
1. Independence. Of the papers read, DESpace2 is the one that says in so many words that treating spots as independent replicates inflates false positives. SpatialDM's analytical null assumes spots are independent within each gene (Supplementary Note 1). SpatialDE and SVCA model spatial covariance among locations but assume independent residual noise and Gaussian errors after transformation. GASTON and Belayer treat counts as independent Poisson given a fitted function.
2. Multiplicity. Across the pairs, genes or interactions: FDR is the norm (SpatialDE Q-values, SVCA Benjamini-Hochberg, BATCOM FDR, DESpace2 Benjamini-Hochberg, SpatialDM FDR < 0.1). Across locations within one map: SpatialDM local spots are called at per-spot p < 0.1 (described as uncorrected in a supplementary figure legend); COMMOT and spacia report no such correction in the text read; GASTON thresholds slope p at 0.1 without a described correction. None of the nine constructs simultaneous bands or excursion sets in the text read.
3. Uncertainty on fields. None of the nine reports a posterior or interval for a spatial expression or signaling field. BATCOM and spacia report posteriors for parameters; BATCOM reduces its posterior to a Wald-type pseudo-p-value.
4. Between-condition signaling. SpatialDM compares per-pair z-scores across slices (replicated, slice level); DESpace2 compares spatial patterns across conditions with pseudobulk replicates. Neither addresses a single-section comparison of regions with correlated bins.
5. What limits our claims: (a) SpatialDM and DESpace2 do cover condition comparisons, so avoid "no method compares conditions"; (b) BATCOM and spacia are Bayesian CCC models, so avoid "CCC methods are not model-based"; (c) several supplements (COMMOT Supplementary Note, spacia Supplementary Files, SpatialDE Supplementary Note, SVCA and DESpace supplements, GASTON and Belayer supplements) were not read, so claims of absence should be phrased "in the main text and methods".
