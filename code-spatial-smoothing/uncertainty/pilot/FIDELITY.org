Caveat that applies to both: the joint draws come from INLA's approximation to the joint
posterior (Gaussian approximations conditional on hyperparameter integration points), not from
exact posterior sampling. The published summaries carry the same approximation.

* Reproduction check (=pilot/check-refit.R=, results in =pilot/check-refit.csv=)

Pass rule, fixed before running: per bin, dlog = log(refit median) - log(saved median); under
Monte Carlo error alone, z = dlog / (sqrt(2) x 1.2533 x s / sqrt(1000)) should be about N(0, 1),
with s the log-scale posterior sd implied by the saved 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Pass if
|mean z| < 0.2, sd(z) < 1.3 and |median dlog| < 0.02.

Result, 2026-10-16: all 17 genes pass. sd(z) ranges 0.993 to 1.017 and |mean z| is at most 0.077,
consistent with the refit and the published fit differing only by Monte Carlo noise. The median
ratio of refit to saved 95% interval width (log scale) ranges 0.995 to 1.002.

Run time: 48 s (Cd44, alone) to 74 s (four genes in parallel) per gene, fit plus 1,000 joint
draws, single-threaded, on this workstation. The handoff's 140 s per gene is from a different
machine and included the published =predict()= of three quantities.

* Finding that corrects an earlier statement

The fitted Matern range (posterior mean, PC parameterization, in chip units; 50 units = 1 bin)
is 469 for Cd44 but 94 to 342 for the other 16 genes, i.e. about 2 to 7 bins. The handoff's
"correlation length about 10 bins" holds for Cd44, not for these genes in general. The boxes
(15 bins on a side) are therefore 2 to 8 ranges across, and the naive bins-as-independent test is
less badly wrong for short-range genes than the plan's wording suggests.
