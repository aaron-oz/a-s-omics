#+TITLE: Notes on four partial precedents for uncertainty-based comparison of spatial expression
#+DATE: 2026-10-14
#+STARTUP: overview

Purpose: reading notes on four papers named as partial precedents for our reframed
paper (R-INLA/inlabru Poisson SPDE fits per gene on one Stereo-seq E16.5 mouse embryo
section, 26,000 bins of 50 x 50 spots, total UMI (unique molecular identifier) count
per bin as offset; joint posterior draws used to compare regions, 1D transects with
simultaneous bands, and whole fields with excursion sets).

Conventions in these notes: "I read" statements describe what was opened in this
session. Anything not read is said to be not read. Quotes are short and verbatim from
the text I extracted. Abbreviations: GLM = general linear model; GMRF = Gaussian Markov
random field; SPDE = stochastic partial differential equation; INLA = integrated nested
Laplace approximation; MCMC = Markov chain Monte Carlo; HMC = Hamiltonian Monte Carlo;
UMI = unique molecular identifier; ST = spatial transcriptomics; LR = ligand-receptor.

* 1. Splotch (Aijo et al., bioRxiv 2019) and Maniatis et al. (Science 2019)

** Citation and what I read
- Aijo T, Maniatis S, Vickovic S, Kang K, Cuevas M, Braine C, Phatnani H, Lundeberg J,
  Bonneau R. "Splotch: Robust estimation of aligned spatial temporal gene expression
  data." bioRxiv 2019, doi:10.1101/757096 (version 1, posted 2019-09-05).
- I read: the bioRxiv v1 full PDF (39 pages), text extracted with pdftotext, including
  the Methods and the supplementary figure legends that are in the PDF. The
  supplementary figures themselves are legends only; the prior definitions are in
  "Supplementary Fig. 1c", which is a figure and whose numbers I did not recover.
- No peer-reviewed journal version found: a web search and a Europe PMC title search
  returned only the preprint (Europe PMC source "PPR"). I cannot rule out one exists.
- Application paper: Maniatis S, Aijo T, Vickovic S, Braine C, Kang K, Mollbrink A,
  Fagegaltier D, Andrusivova Z, Saarenpaa S, Saiz-Castro G, Cuevas M, Watters A,
  Lundeberg J, Bonneau R, Phatnani H. "Spatiotemporal dynamics of molecular pathology
  in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis." Science 364(6435):89-93, 2019,
  doi:10.1126/science.aav9776. I read only the bioRxiv preprint main text (doi
  10.1101/389270, v3, 20 pages). Its Materials and Methods and "Data 1" are listed as
  supplementary and are NOT in that PDF, and the Science version is not open access
  (Europe PMC: not in PMC). So I did not read the Maniatis supplementary methods; the
  model description below comes from the Splotch preprint, which says the core model
  "was used previously" in the ALS paper.
- Software repository read: github.com/tare/Splotch (README and src/splotch/models.py).

** Data type and scale
- Spatial Transcriptomics (Stahl-style array, spots on a lattice; not Stereo-seq).
  Mouse lumbar spinal cord: "76,136 ST spots across 1,165 tissue sections"; mouse main
  olfactory bulb: 3,045 spots, 12 sections. 11,138 genes quantified on the spinal cord
  data. Median per-section depth quoted as 1,415 genes and 2,227 unique transcripts
  per spot before integration. Multiple sections, mice, timepoints, genotypes.

** Model (as written in the preprint Methods)
- Likelihood: zero-inflated Poisson (ZIP), counts n_{g,s,i} for gene g, section s,
  spot i; Poisson-only is the alternative in the code.
- Rate: log-link, exp(lambda_{g,s,i}) = x_i^T beta_{g,f(s)} + phi_{g,s,i} + epsilon_{g,s,i}.
  Three components: (1) beta, a linear model over predefined anatomical annotation
  regions (11 tissue contexts for spinal cord, 5 for olfactory bulb), arranged in a
  hierarchy (genotype+timepoint, then sex, then mouse); (2) phi, a spatial random
  effect per section with a conditional autoregressive (CAR) prior on the 4-neighbor
  lattice graph, with spatial autocorrelation parameter rho_g and conditional
  precision tau_g; (3) epsilon, independent spot-level noise with SD sigma_g.
- Normalization: a non-random exposure ("size factor") s_{s,i} = N_{s,i}/2208, where N
  is the spot's total UMI count and 2,208 is the median depth; the Poisson mean is
  lambda * s. This is the same role as our total-UMI offset.
- Inference: Stan, adaptive HMC, "four independent chains (each with 500 warm-up and
  500 sampling iterations)", R-hat monitored. One gene at a time (the gene index g is
  in every parameter).
- IMPORTANT discrepancy between preprint and current code. The preprint says CAR. The
  README of the repository says "Gaussian Process prior for spatial random effect",
  "NumPyro", "Hilbert space approximate Bayesian Gaussian process". In
  src/splotch/models.py (the version I read) the spatial effect is a Hilbert-space
  approximate GP with a squared-exponential spectral density, with 5 basis functions
  per dimension hard-coded (num_basis = 5), default priors alpha ~ Gamma(2,2),
  length ~ Gamma(10,10), theta ~ Beta(1,2), sigma_spot ~ HalfNormal(0.3), and the
  region coefficient prior beta_level_1 = 2.0 * Normal(0,1). So the code is a
  different spatial model from the one in the preprint. The code samples with NumPyro
  NUTS (run_nuts) or SVI (run_svi).

** Uncertainty produced and how it is used
- Posterior samples of beta (log-scale region coefficients at each level of the
  design), of phi, and of spot-level rates lambda, per gene.
- Comparison rule: "Detection of differential expression between conditions and
  regions was done by quantitating element-wise differences in the estimated posterior
  distributions of the random variables beta using the Savage-Dickey density ratio as
  described previously" (Methods, Analysis of ST data). Figures report a Bayes factor
  for, e.g., ventral horn versus the combination of medial grey and dorsal horn
  (Fig. 1 legend, Lcp1 and Ngb).
- So: the compared quantity is the region-level coefficient beta (a fixed effect for a
  predefined annotation region), not an average of the posterior spatial field over a
  region, and not a transect or an excursion set. The comparison is on marginal
  posteriors of a contrast for one gene and one region pair. Multiplicity handling
  across genes or region pairs: not stated in what I read. The Maniatis preprint main
  text figure legends show "posterior difference distributions" for human tissue
  (Fig. 4A) but I did not see a decision rule beyond that in the main text.
- Splotch uses the spatial terms to improve estimates and to share information across
  sections; the uncertainty of the spatial field itself is not used for comparisons in
  anything I read.

** Ligand-receptor or signaling
- None computed in either text I read. The Splotch preprint only says the methods are
  matched to ST for "interrogating spatiotemporal dynamics of diseases, cell-cell
  communication, and regulatory dynamics" (Main, last paragraph). The Maniatis
  preprint discusses TREM2/TYROBP and APOE biologically; it computes no signaling
  fields. Splotch also builds gene co-expression modules by Pearson correlation of
  posterior-mean rates across genes (Methods, "Spatiotemporal and disease-dependent
  co-expression analysis"), which uses posterior means only.

** Naive comparators and evidence about ignoring spatial correlation
- The preprint's comparator is a "spatially uninformed approach": counts divided by
  per-spot total UMI and rescaled by the median. Fig. 1b reports Pearson correlation
  of its estimates against Splotch's as a function of detection rate; the text says
  they agree for high-signal genes (Mbp, Snap25) and that the uninformed approach
  "fails to capture expression localization predicted by Splotch of lowly expressed
  genes" later checked by protein and in situ images (Lcp1, Ngb). That is evidence
  about point-estimate quality in low-count genes, not about false-positive rates.
- It also compares with SpatialDE and trendsceek for spatially variable gene
  detection: "271 of the 6100 (4.4%) q-values are <= 0.05" for SpatialDE, attributed
  to "low SNR and insufficient spatial sampling" (this is a detection rate, not a
  false-positive rate; there is no false-positive simulation in what I read).
- No test that treats bins as independent is argued against, and no quantitative
  statement that ignoring spatial correlation inflates false positives, in what I read.

** Software
- Python (JAX/NumPyro), BSD-3 license, repository created 2018-08-07, last push
  2025-05-19 in the GitHub API (recent commits I saw were dependency bumps by a bot),
  23 stars, 1 open issue at the time of reading. pip package name "splotch-st" per the
  README.
- Inputs: one tab-separated count file per section (genes by spots, with spot
  coordinates as "x_y" column names); one tab-separated one-hot annotation file per
  section assigning each spot to exactly one anatomical region ("we do not currently
  support more than one annotation category per ST spot"; unannotated spots are
  dropped); a metadata table with a mandatory categorical column level_1 (and level_2,
  level_3 for two- and three-level designs). Run time: not stated in what I read.
- Could it be run on our data? My judgment, not tested: technically plausibly yes for
  one gene, as a one-section, one-level design with spots as bins and region labels we
  supply (fixed boxes or published anatomical regions as the annotation). It would
  give a Poisson (or ZIP) fit with a UMI size factor, a region coefficient per region,
  a smooth spatial effect, and per-bin noise, and a posterior for differences of
  region coefficients. Caveats that would limit it as a comparator for the same
  quantity: (a) the estimand is a region fixed effect, which is not the region
  average of lambda(s) from a spatial field; (b) a 5 x 5 basis GP is much coarser
  than a Matern SPDE mesh on a 200 x 130 grid, and the extra iid bin-level noise term
  adds 26,000 latent variables; (c) regions are annotations (hard assignment per bin)
  so regions cannot overlap; (d) per-gene HMC cost at 26,000 bins is unknown to me and
  would need a timing test; (e) I have not run it, and the code differs from the
  preprint, so a result would be a verdict on the repository's HSGP variant only.
- A faithful "Splotch" comparison would require the preprint's CAR/ZIP Stan model,
  which I did not find as a maintained package (the maintained repository is the
  NumPyro re-implementation described above).

** Similarity and difference to our approach (factual)
- Similar: per-gene count model with a UMI-based exposure, log link, Bayesian
  posterior, a spatially structured random effect, comparison of anatomical regions
  using posteriors.
- Different: multi-section hierarchical design is the point of the method; the
  regional comparison is through coefficients of fixed region effects; MCMC (HMC)
  rather than INLA; zero-inflation and iid spot noise; no joint posterior of the field
  used for credible regions, transects, or excursion sets; no multiplicity or
  simultaneous-band logic in what I read.

* 2. Mejia, Yue, Bolin, Lindgren, Lindquist (JASA 2020; BayesfMRI)

** Citation and what I read
- Mejia AF, Yue YR, Bolin D, Lindgren F, Lindquist MA. "A Bayesian general linear
  modeling approach to cortical surface fMRI data analysis." Journal of the American
  Statistical Association 115(530):501-520, 2020, doi:10.1080/01621459.2019.1611582.
- I read: arXiv:1706.00959 version 1 (stat.AP, submitted 2017-06-03), the full 35+ page
  text with simulation and application sections and discussion, text extracted with
  pdftotext. arXiv lists only v1. I did NOT read the JASA version, so I cannot say
  what changed between this 2017 preprint and the 2020 article. The appendices
  (proof of equation 12, supplementary figures) I did not examine closely.
- Software: CRAN page for BayesfMRI (version 0.11.0, published 2025-12-22) and
  github.com/mandymejia/BayesfMRI (mentioned in the CRAN URL field; I did not open the
  repository itself).

** Data type and scale
- Cortical surface fMRI (functional magnetic resonance imaging): a triangular mesh,
  "approximately 30,000 vertices in each hemisphere" (Introduction); Human Connectome
  Project motor task data, 20 randomly sampled subjects, 284 time points per subject,
  hemisphere resampled to about 6,000 vertices (mesh about 5,300 vertices). A
  simulation study uses a 46 x 55 phantom with 1,256 voxels and T = 200.

** Model
- Likelihood: Gaussian time-series regression y = sum_k X_k beta_k + sum_j Z_j b_j +
  epsilon, with AR(p) errors in the simulation and prewhitening plus independent
  errors in the application. NOT a count model; no offset concept.
- Spatial prior: each activation field beta_k has an SPDE (Matern, alpha = 2, so nu = 1
  in 2D) prior via the finite-element GMRF of Lindgren et al. (2011); hyperparameters
  log kappa and log tau each get independent N(0,1) priors ("log(kappa) ~ N(0,1) and
  log(tau) ~ N(0,1)").
- Inference: INLA through R-INLA, chosen over variational Bayes, which they say
  "tend to severely underestimate posterior variance in latent Gaussian models".
- Multi-subject: a joint modeling approach in which subject-level Gaussian full
  conditionals are combined and the hyperparameters are integrated by importance
  sampling from a Gaussian approximation q(theta | y); also a two-level approach that
  refits INLA on posterior samples.

** Uncertainty and decision rule (the part closest to our design)
- Quantity: joint posterior of each latent field, in the INLA mixture form pi(f | y) =
  sum_l lambda_l pi(f | y, theta_l).
- Decision rule: the positive excursion set E+_{gamma,alpha}(f) = argmax_D {|D| :
  P(D subset of A+_gamma(f)) >= 1 - alpha}, "the largest set of vertices such that
  with at least probability 1 - alpha the level gamma is exceeded at all locations in
  that set" (Section 2.3), computed with the R package excursions (Bolin and
  Lindgren), via its R-INLA interface. They call this the joint posterior probability
  map (joint PPM) and report the excursion function F_gamma(u).
- Joint versus pointwise: they contrast it with the marginal PPM, D = {u : P(f(u) >
  gamma) > 1 - alpha}, and say marginal use "introduces a multiple comparisons
  problem" and that the probability the whole region is active "may be less than
  1 - alpha". Abstract: the excursion method "eliminates the need for multiple
  comparisons correction".
- Thresholds: gamma = 0 and gamma = 1 percent of baseline signal were used.
- Not done in this paper: region averages (linear functionals) of the field, 1D
  transects, or comparisons of two fields' difference; it is detection of where a
  single field exceeds a level. Group-level inference is by the group-average linear
  combination beta_G = A beta, whose joint Gaussian conditional is available in
  closed form.

** Ligand-receptor or signaling
- None.

** Naive comparator and evidence
- Comparator: the classical voxel-wise ("massive univariate") GLM with 6 mm FWHM
  (full width at half maximum) smoothing, t-tests at every vertex, with false
  discovery rate control (Benjamini-Yekutieli) or family-wise error control by
  permutation (max-t). These are multiplicity-corrected, not "independent bins without
  correction". Simulation (one simulated data set as far as I could tell; replicates
  not stated in what I read), significance 0.01: false positive rate / false negative
  rate for activation 1: joint PPM 0.56% / 0.08%, marginal PPM 1.11% / 0.00%, FDR
  1.51% / 1.59%, FWER 1.35% / 1.91%; activation 2: joint 0.16% / 0.88%, marginal
  0.64% / 0.48%, FDR 0.48% / 3.58%, FWER 0.24% / 3.74% (Figure 7 caption). Areas under
  the ROC curve: Bayesian above 0.998; classical 0.913 and 0.874 (FDR), 0.942 and
  0.931 (FWER).
- Point stated in the text: joint and marginal PPMs give identical ROC curves because
  the excursion function is a monotone transformation of the marginal probabilities
  in the family used by the excursions package; the difference is in how values
  cluster near 0 and 1 and so how thresholds behave and how false positives are
  controlled. The marginal PPM gave about twice or more the false positive rate of the
  joint PPM in that one simulation.
- In the HCP data the FWER-corrected classical maps are described as "very
  conservative".

** Software
- R package BayesfMRI, CRAN, GPL-3, version 0.11.0 (2025-12-22), maintained by Mandy
  Mejia; depends on ciftiTools, excursions, fMRItools; INLA in Suggests; NeedsCompilation
  yes. Input is task fMRI in CIFTI format with a cortical surface; the model is
  Gaussian time series.
- Could it run on our data? No, in my judgment: the likelihood is Gaussian and
  built around fMRI design matrices and CIFTI inputs; there is no Poisson-with-offset
  mode in the CRAN description I read. What carries over is the method, not the
  package: R-INLA fit, SPDE prior, joint posterior, and the excursions package, which
  our own pipeline can call directly (excursions accepts INLA results and Gaussian
  samples).

** Similarity and difference
- Very similar in machinery: R-INLA, Matern SPDE field, joint posterior and
  excursion sets with the excursions package, explicit contrast of joint versus
  marginal (pointwise) thresholds, and explicit statement that INLA's posterior
  variance is preferable to variational Bayes.
- Different: Gaussian fMRI time series versus Poisson counts with an offset; mesh on
  a cortical surface versus a regular 200 x 130 grid; target is detection of an
  activated region above a level, not comparison of two regions' averages or
  transects; their comparator is voxel-wise GLM with multiplicity correction.

* 3. SHADE (Eliason, Peruzzi, Rao; PLOS Computational Biology 2026)

** Citation and what I read
- Eliason J, Peruzzi M, Rao A. "SHADE: A multilevel Bayesian framework for modeling
  directional spatial interactions in tissue microenvironments." PLoS Comput Biol
  22(2):e1013930, 2026, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013930 (received 2025-07-31,
  accepted 2026-01-19, published 2026-02-04).
- I read: the full open-access journal HTML text (journals.plos.org, extracted to
  text; math was dropped by my extraction so equations are read from the prose only),
  plus the supplementary text file S1 Text (PDF, 2,792 lines) for the section on
  simultaneous credible bands (D.1) and parts of the simulation description (I read
  D.1 closely and skimmed the rest; I did not read the colorectal-cancer
  supplementary results in detail). SHADE stands for Spatial Hierarchical Asymmetry via
  Directional Estimation.
- Repository: github.com/jeliason/SHADE (README read); analysis code in
  github.com/jeliason/shade_paper_code (not opened).

** Data type and scale
- NOT gene expression. Multiplexed imaging (e.g., multiplexed immunofluorescence)
  point patterns of annotated cell types in tissue. Application: a colorectal cancer
  data set of 35 patients, 140 images, 8 cell types. Timing experiment on simulated
  cells: "At 100,000 cells, total fitting time was approximately 36 seconds; at
  250,000 cells, approximately 133 seconds" (via variational inference, per Section 2.5).

** Model
- Conditional inhomogeneous Poisson point process for a target cell type given
  source cell types; log intensity = intercept + covariates + sum over source types of
  basis-expanded functions of distance to source cells. The spatial interaction curve
  (SIC) is the resulting function of distance s. Hierarchy: image within patient within
  cohort with partial pooling.
- Likelihood is computed by a logistic-regression approximation with dummy points
  (Baddeley et al. 2014), "rather than direct Poisson likelihood estimation".
- Inference: Stan HMC via cmdstanr (variational inference for the timing test).
  Hyperpriors: in S1 Text Section F; not read.

** Uncertainty and decision rule
- Quantity: posterior of the SIC, a 1D function of distance over [r_min, r_max].
- Simultaneous bands: S1 Text D.1. For posterior draws j, standardized residual
  Z^(j)(s) = (SIC^(j)(s) - posterior mean(s)) / posterior SD(s); T^(j) = sup over s of
  |Z^(j)(s)|; critical value c = the (1 - alpha) empirical quantile of T^(j); band =
  posterior mean +/- c * posterior SD for all s. Main text: "simultaneous 95% credible
  bands that account for multiple comparisons across the distance domain. Unlike
  pointwise credible intervals, simultaneous bands provide joint coverage across all
  distances".
- Decision rules: an interaction is called when the band excludes zero somewhere in a
  distance range (detection test in simulations: "whether 95% simultaneous credible
  bands ... excluded zero at any distance"). Group comparison: "Differences between
  groups are then assessed by comparing the posterior distributions of these
  cohort-level parameters, as quantified through SICs ... and their associated
  simultaneous credible bands". In the application they report that "simultaneous 95%
  credible bands overlap at all distances for all cell type pairs" for the two
  immune-phenotype groups and treat suggestive trends as "strictly
  hypothesis-generating". (My observation, not the authors' statement: overlap of two
  separate groups' bands is not the same as a band for their difference; I did not see
  a band on the difference in the main text.)
- Multiple cell-type pairs: prioritization summaries (peak location and magnitude,
  persistence, overall strength) rather than formal multiplicity correction across
  pairs, as I read S1 Text D.2.
- This is the closest of the four to our 1D-transect idea: a simultaneous band built
  from the maximum standardized deviation of joint posterior draws. It is a
  sup-norm band centered at the posterior mean, assembled from MCMC draws, not an
  excursions-package (Gaussian orthant probability) construction.

** Ligand-receptor or signaling
- None. The Discussion says "the SICs reflect spatial dependence rather than
  molecular signaling pathways".

** Naive comparators and evidence
- Comparators: G-cross and K-cross (L-cross) envelope tests with 99 complete spatial
  randomness simulations, and a "Flat" non-hierarchical version of SHADE. These are
  about point-pattern summaries and pooling, not about bins treated as independent.
- Simulation results, with their own context (40 patients, 1 to 3 images per patient,
  50 replicates per condition): SHADE Flat had "poor coverage (73%) regardless of
  scenario and ... severely inflated type I error rates (28%)"; hierarchical SHADE had
  median type I error 0.8 to 7.5% with 2 to 3 images per patient. With unmodeled
  tissue compartments and both cell types abundant, SHADE had type I error 11.7 to
  17.1% and coverage 43 to 52% ("incorrectly attributing compartment effects to
  source-target interactions"). With only 1 image per patient the method "exhibits
  extreme behavior". These are findings about pooling and about unmodeled spatial
  heterogeneity, not a head-to-head on spatial correlation versus independence for
  expression data.

** Software
- R package SHADE (GitHub, jeliason/SHADE; last push 2026-02-20 in the GitHub API,
  2 stars), requires CmdStan through cmdstanr. Inputs: x, y coordinates, cell_type,
  image_id, and a patient metadata table with Spot, Patient, Group.
- Could it be run on our data? No, in my judgment: it needs point patterns of
  labeled cells, and our data are counts on a regular bin grid with no cell
  segmentation or cell-type labels. One could force-fit with discretized "types" but
  that would not be a comparator for the same question. What is reusable is the band
  construction in S1 Text D.1, which is a few lines applied to posterior draws.

** Similarity and difference
- Similar: full posterior draws used to build a simultaneous band over a 1D domain
  with an explicit contrast against pointwise intervals; band excludes zero as the
  decision rule; group comparison via posterior of group-level parameters.
- Different: object is a distance-interaction curve of cell types, not an expression
  field; HMC not INLA; hierarchical across images and patients; no regions, no
  whole-field excursion sets, no expression offsets, no ligand-receptor quantities.

* 4. SpaceBF (Seal and Neelon; bioRxiv 2025, GigaScience 2026)

** Citation and what I read
- Seal S, Neelon B. "SpaceBF: Spatial coexpression analysis using Bayesian Fused
  approaches in spatial omics datasets." bioRxiv 2025, doi:10.1101/2025.03.29.646124
  (version posted 2025-04-03). Published as a GigaScience Technical Note,
  GigaScience 15:giag006, 2026, doi:10.1093/gigascience/giag006 (PMC12954175).
- I read: (a) the bioRxiv v1 full PDF (44 pages), main text, methods, references; the
  Supplementary Material it refers to ("Theorem 1", more discussion of the prior) is
  NOT in that PDF; (b) the GigaScience full text via the Europe PMC XML (open
  access), main text and methods; the OUP page itself returned a bot-block stub, and
  the Supplementary Material files were not read. Math in the XML was stripped by my
  extraction, so symbols and some numbers (for example the exact MCMC counts) were
  recovered by hand from the XML.
- Software: github.com/sealx017/SpaceBF (README, DESCRIPTION, R/SpaceBF_main.R read).

** Data type and scale
- Spatial transcriptomics (the older Spatial Transcriptomics array): a cutaneous
  melanoma section with 293 spots (100 micrometer spots at 200 micrometer
  center-to-center), 16,148 genes; a cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma section with
  621 spots; plus, in the published version, a MALDI (matrix-assisted laser
  desorption/ionization) mass spectrometry imaging section of ductal carcinoma in
  situ. Single sample each. Runtime benchmark up to n = 5,000 locations.

** Model
- Pairwise spatially varying coefficient (SVC) regression: gene m is the outcome and
  gene m' is a predictor, with spatially varying intercept beta0(s) and slope beta1(s)
  plus optional covariates (e.g., cell type). Count outcome: negative binomial (NB)
  with logit-form failure probability and constant dispersion r; the predictor
  enters as its observed count (the authors note "the count-valued nature of the
  molecule ... is not prioritized, appearing as a spatially varying predictor"). A
  Gaussian version also exists.
- Spatial prior: a "fused horseshoe" GMRF prior on differences of coefficients across
  edges of a graph, by default the minimum spanning tree (MST) of the locations
  (random tie-breaking on regular grids); half-Cauchy global and local scales; setting
  local scales to one recovers an intrinsic CAR (ICAR) prior. The published version
  also compares HS and ICAR priors on MST, Delaunay and k-nearest-neighbor graphs and
  sdmTMB Matern (SPDE, Laplace-approximate maximum likelihood) fits.
- Normalization or offset: none in the model as written; the function SpaceBF() in
  R/SpaceBF_main.R has arguments y1, y2, X, G, which.model, which.prior, nIter, etc.;
  I found no offset argument (grep for "offset" in the NB sampler files returned
  nothing). A log total-UMI term could in principle be passed as a covariate in X but
  that is a fixed effect, not an offset, and I did not test it.
- Inference: Gibbs sampler with Polya-Gamma augmentation (NB), sparse Cholesky via
  the spam package. In the published version: "In most analyses, we ran 5,000 MCMC
  iterations with 2,500 burn-in" (numbers recovered from the XML).

** Uncertainty and decision rules
- Quantity: posterior of beta1(s) at each spot, and of its tissue average.
- Global test: credible interval and probability of direction (pd, the posterior
  proportion sharing the sign of the median) of the average slope over all locations,
  reported as p = 2(1 - pd). Local test: "directly based on the credible intervals of
  [the] beta1(s)'s", that is, pointwise per spot. Multiplicity: "For FDR control, we
  apply the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure using the p.adjust function in R" (published
  Methods), which in the application is across gene pairs for the global test (for
  example in the preprint: 53 LR pairs at level 0.05 and 33 at FDR < 0.1 in
  melanoma). I did not see any joint (simultaneous or excursion-based) treatment of
  the local, per-spot tests; they are marginal.
- Not compared: regions, transects, or excursion sets of the field. Regions are used
  only descriptively (clustering slope surfaces and comparing to pathologist regions).

** Ligand-receptor or signaling
- Yes, as an application: the melanoma analysis tests 161 LR pairs (of 1,180 in
  CellChatDB, after filtering genes with fewer than 0.2 x n reads) by regressing
  ligand count and receptor count against each other with spatially varying slope.
  The signaling quantity is a coefficient of association, not a min of rate fields.
  It is a different definition from ours (pointwise minimum of ligand and receptor
  rate fields).

** Naive comparators and evidence about spatial correlation
- Comparators: Pearson correlation, MERINGUE, SpatialDM, Lee's L, SpaGene (all
  permutation or exact tests on a global statistic). The authors report that
  "even when two variables are independently simulated with certain spatial covariance
  structures, the unmodeled spatial autocorrelation introduces a confounding effect on
  the bivariate association, leading to significantly inflated type 1 error rates".
  Results text (simulation design 1, n = 293 locations of the melanoma sample, Gaussian
  copula NB data with exponential-kernel spatial correlation, lengthscale l = 3.6):
  SpaceBF "maintains the correct type 1 error" while "all other methods suffer from
  inflated type 1 errors", Pearson being the least inflated, Lee's L the most, and the
  inflation grows with l. I did not extract numeric error rates; they are in figures
  (preprint Fig. 3 and 4) which I did not inspect as images.
- This is the one paper of the four with an explicit simulation of false-positive
  inflation from ignoring spatial autocorrelation, but it concerns the association
  between two genes, not a difference between regions.

** Software
- R package SpaceBF, GitHub sealx017/SpaceBF; version 0.2.0 in DESCRIPTION; last push
  2026-06-17 in the GitHub API; 5 stars; README updates dated 2025-10-16 and
  2026-04-05; installed with devtools::install_github; imports include BayesLogit,
  MERINGUE, SpaGene, spam, igraph, bayestestR, coda, ComplexHeatmap and others, so the
  install is heavy. The preprint said the package "will be made available soon"; the
  repository now exists.
- Runtime reported (published version): "For n = 5,000, SpaceBF completes in about 20
  min on a Mac Pro (M3 Max)" and "scaling approximately linearly with n" (Figure 10,
  not inspected as an image), for one gene pair. Complexity statement in the
  Discussion: near linear on trees, "typically around" n^1.5 for planar graphs (my
  reading of the stripped math; check before quoting). They list VI as future work to
  "improve scalability".
- Could it be run on our data? Partly, with a different estimand: for one gene pair
  it takes two count vectors over 26,000 bins and an adjacency matrix and returns
  slope-surface posteriors. Rough extrapolation, not a measurement: if the reported
  linear scaling held, 26,000 bins would take on the order of 5 times the 20 min
  reported for 5,000, per pair, on a fast laptop; 790 genes would mean 790 x 789 / 2
  pairs if all pairs were wanted, so a pair-level run is only feasible for a short list
  of ligand-receptor pairs. It cannot produce our estimand: no total-UMI offset in
  the interface, the answer is a spatially varying regression slope rather than
  min(ligand rate, receptor rate), and the local inference is pointwise. It could
  serve as a comparator for "is this ligand-receptor association credible" with the
  differences stated, not for region comparisons of signaling fields.

** Similarity and difference
- Similar: count likelihood for ST, Bayesian spatial prior (a GMRF), posteriors with
  credible intervals, ligand-receptor application, an explicit argument that
  ignoring spatial autocorrelation inflates false positives.
- Different: association regression of one gene on another, not rate fields with an
  offset; MCMC Gibbs, not INLA; ST arrays with hundreds of spots, not 26,000 bins;
  per-spot tests pointwise; no joint region, transect or excursion treatment.
