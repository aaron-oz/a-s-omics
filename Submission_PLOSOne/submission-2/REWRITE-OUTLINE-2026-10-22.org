* The message, in one paragraph

Signaling fields estimated from spatial transcriptomics are usually read from point-estimate
maps. Our model gives every gene field, and therefore every ligand-receptor signaling field, a
joint posterior. Using it, we ask whether differences that are visible on the maps are supported
by the data: between regions (fixed boxes and published anatomical regions), along line
transects, and over whole fields. On one E16.5 mouse embryo section, many visible differences are
not supported once the posterior uncertainty, and in particular its spatial correlation, is taken
into account, and a smaller number of differences that are not visible are supported, mostly with
small effect sizes. A test that treats bins as independent agrees with the map far more often
than the posterior does. These are demonstrations of what the framework allows on one section,
not biological findings.

* Abstract (skeleton, not prose)

1. Problem: signaling fields are read by eye from point estimates; uncertainty is rarely
   propagated, and bins are spatially correlated.
2. Method: per-gene Poisson-SPDE models with a total-count offset; joint posterior draws; the
   posterior of each common-minimum signaling field; comparisons over regions, transects, whole
   fields; joint inference by excursion sets.
3. Data: one field of view, E16.5 mouse embryo, Stereo-seq, 790 genes, 1,477 ligand-receptor
   pairs.
4. Results (numbers at T = 1.25; 1.5 and 2 in the text): of 19,201 region comparisons, 11,527
   look different on the published maps and 3,603 of those are not credible; a bin-independent
   Wilcoxon test calls 90% of comparisons different, the posterior with spatial correlation
   removed 73%, the joint posterior 46%; small and same-tissue regions carry most of the
   unsupported readings.
5. Scope sentence: one section, one platform; the inferred fields estimate the potential for
   signaling under the co-expression assumption, not signaling itself.

** Materials and methods

Keep (light edits): ligand-receptor principles, spatial modeling principles, the model
specification, dataset selection and preprocessing, the three-likelihood comparison.

New subsections:
1. Joint posterior draws: refits with the published specification, 1,000 joint draws per gene;
   the fidelity table in brief and the reproduction check (778 of 790 genes within Monte Carlo
   noise; 12 sparse genes differ, 17 pairs, flagged and reported both ways).
2. Posterior of a signaling field: M(s) = min of the two rate fields, per draw; rate scale;
   independence of ligand and receptor posteriors.
3. Regions and transects: five 15 x 15-bin boxes, five MOSTA anatomical regions, 13 frozen
   comparisons; four transects; all fixed before any posterior comparison (frozen design,
   dated).
4. Quantities: areal-average ratios; shape normalization for cross-field comparisons; deviation
   from the transect average; whole-field contrasts at bins and at 5 x 5-bin blocks.
5. Inference: 95% intervals for scalar contrasts; excursion sets and simultaneous bands for
   transects and whole fields; pointwise intervals as secondary.
6. Readings we compare against: "looks different" (plug-in ratio >= T, T = 1.25 from author
   rulings on five examples, with 1.5 and 2); Wilcoxon tests treating bins as independent; the
   posterior with between-bin correlation removed (draws shuffled across bins).
7. Showcase selection: three pairs drawn at random under a fixed rule, the four Fig 5 pairs,
   Cd44, PKM-CD44; all chosen before their posteriors were computed.

Shrink: the convolution-operator comparison and the hyperfield-clustering methods move to a

** Discussion

1. What the uncertainty adds, stated with the counts and their limits.
2. Why bins-as-independent fails here (fitted correlation ranges of about 2 to 9 bins for the showcase genes, longer for some sparse genes; the decorrelated
   comparator).
3. Limits: one section, one platform; INLA's approximation; the plug-in bias; "looks different"
   as a proxy for eyeballing; counts share regions and genes, so they are not rates; 12 genes
   whose refits differ from the published fit.
4. Future work: cluster-level inference in the fMRI sense (with a null that a single section
   cannot supply by permutation), multiple sections, other platforms; the existing transfer
   paragraph.
5. Relationship to existing methods: keep, add the four precedents above.
