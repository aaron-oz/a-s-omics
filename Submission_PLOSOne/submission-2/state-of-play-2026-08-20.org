#+TITLE: Where the revision stands, and the one experiment that would most change it
#+DATE: 2026-08-20

Written for the AOZ/MSBR discussion, 2026-08-20. Terms are defined at first use so this can
be read cold.

* Two measures used throughout

- *Spatial coherence score* (ELSA). Lower is better: domains are cleaner and less mixed at
  their edges. This is the paper's own internal measure.
- *Agreement with reference anatomy* (adjusted Rand index). 0 is chance, 1 is perfect. This
  compares our domains to the anatomical labels Chen et al. published for the same section,
  which our pipeline never saw. This is an external measure and is the more trustworthy of
  the two.

* Not in question

- The model is sound. Poisson beats the zero-inflated and zero-adjusted alternatives on all
  four scores across 912 genes. The fitted zero-inflation for Cd44 is 0.07%, so the extra
  machinery is measurably unnecessary at this sequencing depth.
- The domains are real. Permuting the spot coordinates and refitting everything collapses
  them: 20 domains become 4 to 12, coherence goes from 0.075 to 0.470, with no overlap
  between the real value and any of 49 permutations.
- The domains are anatomically meaningful. Agreement with the published annotation is 0.401
  against a null in which all 499 permutations fell below 0.0011.
- Reproducibility is now excellent, and better than the manuscript claims. With thread count
  and seed fixed the pipeline is bit-identical across two machines and two library versions,
  and an independently written script reproduced the published full-depth numbers to four
  decimal places.

* What we found that is uncomfortable

These are four separate lines of evidence and they all point the same way.

- *Scrambling which ligand goes with which receptor changes nothing.* 99 scrambled pairings,
  with every accidental real pair repaired out, produce domains just as coherent as the true
  pairing. 24 of the 99 did better than the real one. Replicated in a second clustering
  implementation, where 17 of 19 scrambles did better.
- *The combination step appears to cost us rather than help.* Shelved data from July, two
  independent runs, clustering the ligand and receptor maps directly and skipping the
  combination altogether:

  | representation                    | agreement with anatomy | second run |
  |-----------------------------------+------------------------+------------|
  | no combination, L and R maps only |                  0.447 |      0.492 |
  | product                           |                  0.438 |      0.471 |
  | common minimum (what we use)      |                  0.379 |      0.350 |

  Skipping the step wins on both measures, and the operator we chose comes last.
- *The operator is visibly lossy.* Taking the smaller of the two maps returns the dimmer
  partner almost everywhere: for 42.6% of mechanisms the same partner wins at more than 95%
  of locations, and 28 mechanisms collapse to maps numerically identical to another
  mechanism's. So many "interaction fields" are relabelled single-gene maps.
- *We compute uncertainty and then throw it away.* The combination step uses the middle of
  each posterior, not the spread. Already corrected in the manuscript text.

* Why this matters

Steps 1, 3 and 4 of the paper survive intact: model the counts, cluster the result, show the
domains are real and anatomical. It is step 2, combining a ligand map with its receptor map,
that is in trouble, and step 2 is what the title and framing are about.

* The one experiment that would most change this

There is a plausible rescue, and it is cheap. A separate analysis found that the pairing
signal is invisible on raw counts but strong once the two partners are put on a common scale
before combining them. Gene abundances span orders of magnitude, so "take the smaller of the
two" is currently answering "which gene is dimmer" rather than "where are both present".
Rescale first and it becomes a real "both must be high" test.

*Proposal: one run, eight conditions.* Four representations (no combination, product,
geometric mean, common minimum) crossed with two input treatments (as-is, rescaled), all
clustered identically and scored on both measures.

- Costs nothing to set up: no model refitting, the fitted maps already exist.
- Rough estimate, extrapolated from measured timings rather than measured directly: about a
  day of unattended cluster time including replicate seeds.
- *If rescaling wins:* the paper's central step is vindicated with a specific, defensible
  fix, and the finding is more interesting than the original claim.
- *If it does not:* we report honestly that the combination step does not improve on the
  ligand and receptor maps in this dataset, which is a real and useful negative result for a
  field that assumes otherwise.

Either way we learn which paper we have. Right now we do not know.

* Decisions

1. Do we report the pairing-scramble result? The response letter already tells the reviewers
   this experiment could be run to a conclusion. It has been run.
2. Do we run the eight-condition experiment before resubmitting?
3. If the answer to 2 is no, does the paper retreat to "here is a well-controlled modelling
   framework and here is honestly what it does and does not show"?

* Smaller corrections, already identified, no decision needed

- The Figure 6 caption says domains become more numerous as features are added. They fall,
  from 33 to about 20.
- The Results say the full feature set gave the greatest coherence. Four shallower depths
  score better.
- There are 1,449 numerically distinct interaction maps, not 1,477.
- The coordinate-shuffle control used a different clustering implementation from the main
  pipeline and the manuscript does not say so. Needs one sentence.
- Adding many maps genuinely does help, but the published curve's shape is an artefact of
  the order features were added. Hundreds are needed rather than tens, and the benefit
  levels off. This replicates in both implementations.
