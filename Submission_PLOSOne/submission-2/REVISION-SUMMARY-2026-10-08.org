* The paper's message after the revision

A Bayesian spatial model fitted to raw transcript counts gives continuous maps, with
uncertainty, of where each ligand and receptor gene is expressed. Combining each ligand map
with its receptor's map and clustering the results recovers tissue domains. The domains are
real (they disappear when the measurements are shuffled across locations) and match the
published anatomy well above chance. But they come from the collection of gene maps, not from
which ligand is paired with which receptor.

So the contribution is the modeling framework and what it lets you check, demonstrated on one
tissue section. It is not a discovery about signaling. The maps estimate where signaling
*could* happen, assuming that a ligand and its receptor expressed in the same place have the
opportunity to interact; they do not measure signaling.

* Terms used below

- *Field* or *map*: a smooth map, fitted by our model, of how much one gene is expressed across
  the tissue section. We fitted 912.
- *Interaction field*: a map for one ligand-receptor pair. The paper makes it with the
  *common minimum*: at each location, take the smaller of the ligand's and the receptor's value.
- *Domains*: the regions found by clustering all the interaction fields together.
- *ELSA*: our measure of how clean the domains are. Lower is cleaner.
- *ARI* (adjusted Rand index): agreement between two ways of dividing up the tissue. 0 is
  chance, 1 is identical.
- *Seurat* is the clustering software behind the paper's figures. A faster stand-in (RcppHNSW
  plus igraph) was used for the expensive controls. Their scores are on different scales.

* The three controls

1. *Shuffle the measurements across locations, refit everything.* Real data: 20 domains, ELSA
   0.075. 49 shuffled versions: 4 to 12 domains, ELSA 0.412 to 0.583. The domains are not
   made by the smoothing. (Clustered with the faster stand-in, now disclosed.)
2. *Compare with the published anatomy.* ARI 0.401; all 499 random relabelings scored 0.001
   or less. Our domains also split some organs into several regions.
3. *Shuffle which receptor goes with which ligand* (no real pair kept). The domains are just as
   clean: in Seurat, 17 of 19 shuffles were cleaner than the true pairing; in the stand-in, 24
   of 99. We read that as no effect of the pairing on the domains.

The pairing still carries information, which is why we describe this as a limit rather than
as "pairing does not matter":

- *Distinct maps.* Sometimes two pairs give an identical map: if they share a partner that is
  the smaller one everywhere, the minimum just returns that partner's map. For example, seven
  GDF9 pairs give one map. The true pairing gives 1,449 distinct maps from 1,477 pairs. Random
  pairings collapse more: across 9,999 random pairings the average was 1,424 distinct maps,
  and only one reached 1,449 or more (p = 0.0002). The first run used only 99 random
  pairings, which can never give a p below 0.01; this count needs no clustering, so the larger
  run took about 35 minutes.
- *Spatial similarity.* True partners are more alike in where they are expressed than random
  partners (correlation 0.111 against 0.068).

Our working explanation, not tested: on raw counts one partner is usually the smaller one
almost everywhere, so the minimum mostly hands the clustering one gene's map, and the pairing
information never reaches it.

* Errors we found in our own paper, all now corrected in the text

1. 918 genes said modeled; it was 912.
2. 1,481 interaction fields; it is 1,477 pairs (four were duplicate input files), which give
   1,449 distinct maps. The duplication is now disclosed.
3. Table 1 was misread: Poisson wins on all four scores.
4. The stretched-pixel plotting bug (Figs 3-6).
5. Fig 6 caption said domains become more numerous with more fields. They become fewer (33
   at 10 fields, about 20 from 350 on).
6. The text said using all fields gave the cleanest domains. Five deeper subsets scored
   slightly better.
7. The text said clustering without principal-component analysis was best. No matched
   comparison exists, so the claim is removed.
8. Fig 5 said the common minimum gave the lowest ELSA of the operators. The geometric mean was
   slightly lower; the minimum gave the most clusters.
9. Uncertainty was said to be carried through; it was not. Now stated, and partly done (below).
10. The coordinate-shuffle control used the stand-in clustering; now disclosed.
11. *New today:* the reproducibility section applied the stand-in's results to Seurat. A single
    thread plus a fixed seed makes the *stand-in* exactly reproducible; for Seurat that was
    never checked. Re-running the published Seurat clustering with an independent script gave
    scores within 0.012 of the published ones (0.1236 against 0.1237 at full depth). The
    section now says exactly that.

* New analyses added to the paper today

- *How many fields are needed.* The published experiment added fields in order of variance,
  mixing "how many" with "which ones". With random subsets instead, domains get steadily
  cleaner up to a few hundred fields and slowly after. The rise and fall in the published
  curve went away, so it seems to come from the ordering. Now in the Results and in Fig 6.
- *The interaction maps against their uncertainty* (S11 Fig, new Results subsection). Each
  published map takes the smaller of the two genes' best estimates (posterior medians). The
  proper quantity is the uncertainty-aware value of the minimum itself, which can be computed
  exactly from the saved model output because the two genes were fitted separately. Results:
  - Compared with that quantity's median, the published maps are never lower (a mathematical
    certainty) and usually close (median 1.3% higher), but at 22% of pair-location
    combinations they are more than 25% higher.
  - The overstatement is largest where neither partner is clearly the smaller one, not where
    uncertainty is largest as we had guessed.
  - Compared with its mean, the published maps are lower in most places, because the model's
    uncertainty is wide and skewed.
  - Either way the spatial pattern barely changes (median correlation 0.988 and 0.967), so we
    expect the domains would barely change. We have not re-clustered to confirm.
  - Caveat: the saved output has summaries, not samples, so each gene's uncertainty was
    approximated by a lognormal curve; checks on the saved summaries support that for most
    locations.
