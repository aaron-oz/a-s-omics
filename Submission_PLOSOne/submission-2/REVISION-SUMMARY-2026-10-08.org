* Summary

All 23 journal and reviewer requests have a written response, and the manuscript edits for
them were made in July and August 2026. While doing that work we re-derived the paper's
results from the archived outputs and ran the controls Reviewer 2 asked for. The controls
support the paper's main claims: the domains are not produced by the smoothing, and they
agree with the published anatomy. The re-derivation also turned up errors in our own
manuscript, some small and some not, plus one result that changes how the paper should
describe what it shows. That result is that the domains do not depend on which ligand is
paired with which receptor.

Where we have landed: resubmit the paper with the errors corrected, that result reported
honestly, and one new analysis that uses the paper's distinctive feature (the uncertainty
estimates). Larger questions the work raised go to a second paper.

* Terms used below

- *Field*: a smooth map, fitted by our statistical model, of how much one gene is expressed
  across the tissue section. We fitted 912 of them.
- *Interaction field*: a map for one ligand-receptor pair, made by combining the ligand's
  field with the receptor's. The paper combines them with the *common minimum*: at each
  location, take the smaller of the two values.
- *Domains*: the regions found by clustering all the interaction fields together.
- *ELSA* (entropy-based local indicator of spatial association): our measure of how clean
  the domains are. Lower is cleaner.
- *ARI* (adjusted Rand index): agreement between two ways of dividing up the tissue. 0 is
  chance, 1 is identical.

* New analyses, and what they found

** The controls Reviewer 2 asked for

- *Coordinate shuffle.* Scramble which location each measurement came from, refit every
  model, cluster again. The real data give 20 domains with ELSA 0.075; 49 shuffled
  versions give 4 to 12 domains with ELSA 0.412 to 0.583. The domains are not something the
  smoothing creates on its own.
- *Agreement with the published anatomy.* Our domains against the anatomical labels Chen et
  al. published for the same section: ARI 0.401, where all 499 random relabelings scored
  0.001 or less. Our domains also split some organs into several regions.

** The clustering was not reproducible, and now is

Running the published clustering twice on identical input gave different answers (19 to 22
domains). The cause was multithreading without a fixed random seed. With one thread and a
fixed seed, repeated runs are identical. An independently written script then reproduced
the published full-set result (ELSA 0.1236 against the published 0.1237).

** Errors we found in our own manuscript

Some are already corrected in the text; the rest are listed under "What is left".

1. We said 918 genes were modeled. It was 912 (three excluded beforehand, three did not fit).
2. We said 1,481 interaction fields. There are 1,477 ligand-receptor pairs; the extra four
   were duplicate copies of input files. Further, the common minimum makes 28 pairs
   numerically identical to another pair's map (for example, seven GDF9 pairs give the
   same map), so there are 1,449 distinct maps.
3. Table 1 was misread: Poisson wins on all four scores.
4. The stretched-pixel plotting bug (R2.6).
5. The Fig 6 caption says domains become more numerous as more fields are added. They
   become fewer: 33 with 10 fields, settling near 20.
6. The text says using all fields gave the cleanest domains. Four smaller sets scored better
   in the same run.
7. We said uncertainty is carried through the pipeline. It is computed, then only the
   best estimate is used. Corrected in the text.
8. The coordinate-shuffle control was clustered with a faster stand-in for the main
   clustering software. Fine for the control (both arms used it) but not disclosed.

** How many fields are needed

The published experiment added fields in order of variance, so "more fields" was mixed up
with "which fields". Rerunning with random sets of fields shows that a few hundred are
needed and the benefit then levels off. The bump in the published curve came from the
ordering, not from the number of fields.

** Does the ligand-receptor pairing matter? Two answers

- *For the domains, no.* Randomly re-pairing receptors with ligands (99 times, with no real
  pair left in) gives domains just as clean as the true pairing: 24 of 99 were cleaner.
  Repeated with the main clustering software, 17 of 19 were cleaner. The domains come from
  the collection of gene maps, not from the pairing.
- *As information, yes.* True pairs give more distinct maps than random ones (1,449 against
  about 1,424; no random set reached 1,449), and true partners are more alike in where they
  are expressed than random partners (correlation 0.111 against 0.068).
- *Our working explanation, not yet tested directly:* on raw counts one partner is usually
  the smaller one almost everywhere (for 42.6% of pairs, at over 95% of locations), so the
  minimum mostly returns that partner's map and the pairing information does not reach the
  clustering.

This matters for how the paper is written. It still shows that the method recovers real,
anatomical structure. It cannot claim that the specific ligand-receptor pairings drive it.

** Explored and set aside

- *Comparing ways of combining or not combining the maps* (the "representation ladder").
  Its clustering did not reproduce the published one (ARI 0.431 against our own published
  labels), so its results cannot be quoted. Not in this paper.
- *A cross-validated score for comparing ways of dividing up the tissue.* Built and
  calibrated; belongs to the second paper.

* Where we have landed, and why

*This resubmission:* the paper as revised, plus four things.

1. The eight errors above corrected.
2. The pair-shuffle result reported in the letter, in the Controls section, and briefly in
   the Discussion, as a limitation of the common minimum on raw counts. Draft text:
   =pair-shuffle-reporting-plan.org=.
3. A scope statement: the maps estimate the *potential* for signaling, assuming
   co-expression; they do not measure signaling.
4. One new result on uncertainty. The pipeline takes the minimum of the two best-estimate
   maps. The minimum of two uncertain quantities is, on average, lower than the minimum of
   their best estimates, so the published maps are biased upward, most where the model is
   least certain. The uncertainty estimates are already saved, so this needs no refitting:
   one computation, one figure, one paragraph.

*Why here and not further.* Every reviewer request is met, and the paper's core claims (a
sound model, real domains, agreement with anatomy) hold up under the controls. The questions
the work raised (whether a different combining rule carries the pairing through, whether
rescaling the maps first helps, a head-to-head with CytoSignal, testing against known
downstream target genes) are a different and larger paper, likely months of work (our rough estimate, not
planned in detail), and
some of them may not come out in our favor. Folding them in would delay the resubmission
further and risk a paper whose conclusions are still moving. The uncertainty result is the
exception because it is cheap and it rests on what is distinctive in our approach: in a
brief literature search (four queries, not a systematic review) we found no other
ligand-receptor method that models these maps with uncertainty.
