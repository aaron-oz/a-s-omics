Dear Dr. D'Acquisto,

Please find attached our revised manuscript, PONE-D-26-04349, originally submitted as "Spatial
Modeling of Tissues for Morphogenic Field Analysis" and now titled "Uncertainty-aware comparison
of ligand-receptor interaction fields estimated from spatial transcriptomics", together with a
point-by-point response to the reviewers, a summary of the revision, and the marked-up manuscript.
We thank you and the reviewers for a careful review, and you for the extensions that made a
thorough revision possible.

We want to be clear at the outset that the revision reframes the paper. Working through the
reviewers' comments on positioning, on controls and baselines, and on the reach of our claims, we
concluded that what our framework offers beyond existing methods is a posterior distribution over
every gene field and every ligand-receptor interaction field, and that the paper should be about
using it. The revised manuscript asks whether differences visible on maps of these fields are
supported by the data, for averages over regions, along transects and over whole fields, and sets
each answer beside the readings commonly made without a posterior. On the tissue section studied,
many differences that look clear on the maps are not supported once the uncertainty, and in
particular its spatial correlation, is accounted for. The tissue-domain analysis of the original
submission is retained in a shorter section, with the controls the reviewers requested. Because the
changes are extensive, the revision summary and the clean manuscript will be easier to read than
the marked-up copy, which we provide as required.

The response letter addresses every comment from the journal, the academic editor and both
reviewers. In summary:

- At Reviewer 2's request we added baselines and controls. Every comparison of interaction fields
  is now set against the point-estimate map, a test that treats locations as independent, and our
  posterior with its spatial correlation removed. For the tissue domains, refitting the whole
  pipeline on spatially permuted data collapses them; they agree well above chance with the
  anatomical annotation published independently for the same section; and shuffling which receptor
  is paired with which ligand does not make them less coherent, a result we report in full with the
  limit it places on our conclusions.
- We positioned the work against the methods the reviewers named and against the closest
  precedents for the new analysis, read in full, and narrowed our contribution statement to the
  combination we offer.
- We rewrote the model specification, added a supplementary table mapping every mathematical
  symbol to the code that implements it, documented the analysis repository for reproduction, and
  built the figures from scripts at the journal's page size.
- In re-deriving our results from the archived outputs we found and corrected several errors of
  our own, including two feature counts, the direction of one figure caption, and two claims in the
  Results that our saved data did not support. These are listed under "Additional corrections" in
  the response letter.
- We have narrowed the paper's claims to match the evidence: the analysis uses one field of view of
  one section, and the inferred fields estimate the potential for signaling rather than measuring
  it.
