* THE HEADLINE CHANGE THIS SESSION

The evidence was previously filed as "five or six converging nulls: the ligand-receptor
pairing does not matter." *That filing was wrong.* Reorganized, it splits into two
questions with opposite answers:

*Does the pairing carry spatial information? Yes, three independent lines.*
- True pairings yield 1,449 numerically distinct interaction fields against a null of about
  1,424 (z = +4.30 substitute, +3.91 Seurat); not one of 99 scrambles reached the observed.
- Effective rank on standardized fields separates true from scrambled at z = +9.69.
- *New 2026-09-07:* true partners are more spatially correlated than dominance-matched
  random partners. Pearson 0.1110 against 0.0675, paired difference +0.0435, p = 4.0e-21,
  true higher in 60.8% of mechanisms. Spearman 0.3646 against 0.3250, p = 3.8e-15.

*Does that information survive the raw-count common minimum into the clustering? No, three
independent lines.*
- Control A coherence, substitute: z = -0.60, and 24 of 99 scrambles beat the true pairing.
- Control A coherence, Seurat: z = +1.73, and 17 of 19 scrambles beat it. Sign flips
  between implementations, which is what noise around a null looks like.
- Effective rank on raw counts: z = -0.51 for the minimum, -1.49 for the product.

*Why this matters.* "Six nulls" is a discouraging paper. "The pairing carries real spatial
signal, and here is the specific mechanism by which the pipeline's operator destroys it"
is a good one. Do not revert to the old framing.

** magnitude-check.R: dominance does not imply duplication

Validated first: the stored interaction fields equal =pmin(L,R)= to 5.3e-15, so the gene
mapping is right.

- Median dominance (fraction of bins where the same partner supplies the minimum) 0.927.
  Above 0.95 for 629 of 1477 (42.6%), reproducing the previously known figure exactly.
- *93 fields have dominance exactly 1.00*, meaning they are numerically identical to a gene
  field already in the 912-gene input. 156 are at r > 0.99, 268 at r > 0.95.
- *But median correlation with the dominant parent is only 0.713*, so the dominant parent
  accounts for about half the variance, not nearly all of it. The hypothesis that the
  minimum is mostly a re-scaled copy of one parent is *refuted*. Even at 96% dominance the
  4% of bins where the other partner wins are spatially clustered, so the min field is the
  dominant parent with regions carved out of it.

*Consequence:* the claim in NEXT-PAPER-DIRECTIONS.md section 2 that "the operator largely
discards one of the two fields" is an overstatement and should be softened. Discarding the
non-limiting partner at each bin is true by construction; "largely one field" is not.

** linear-recoverability.R: the minimum is not linearly recoverable from its parents

This is the measurement that makes the paper's method defensible.

- Median R^2 of =lm(min ~ L + R)= is *0.555*.
- *81.2% of mechanisms* leave more than 10% of variance unexplained by any linear function
  of both parents. Only 9.4% are essentially linear (R^2 > 0.99).
- Adding the second parent linearly gains a median of only *0.016* over the dominant parent
  alone, so essentially all the extra content is nonlinear.
- Split by dominance: median linear R^2 is 0.842 above dominance 0.95 and 0.438 below.

*Why it matters.* Verified in the Fig 6 experiment script: the published clustering path is
=ScaleData= then =FindNeighbors(dims = NULL, features = foi)=, with the comment "in full
feature space, again without any PCA". So the metric is Euclidean on z-scored features and
*linear in its inputs*. It cannot construct the gate itself. AOZ's generalization is
stronger and should be the paper's framing: PCA is near-universal in this literature and no
linear projection can produce a minimum, so the argument does not depend on our pipeline
lacking PCA.

** matched-dominance-scramble.R, then cor-true-vs-matched.R

Nonlinear content is driven mainly by dominance, so the fair null holds dominance constant
and swaps in a random receptor excluded from all 2,954 directed FANTOM5 pairs. Matching was
good: median dominance gap 0.0029, medians 0.9270 against 0.9269.

| statistic                | true   | matched scramble | paired median diff |
| linear R^2 of min ~ L+R  | 0.5552 | 0.5022           | +0.0174            |
| switch coherence         | 0.9574 | 0.9642           | -0.0005            |

Switch coherence is the fraction of adjacent bin pairs with the same limiting partner.
Both arms sit near 0.96 against *0.8123* for a spatially random mask of the same dominance,
so the operator's partner-switching follows smooth spatial domains rather than flickering
bin to bin. *That closes the high-frequency artifact worry*, with the caveat that the input
fields are SPDE-smoothed so some of the coherence is imposed by the fitting.

Both differences run *opposite* to the hypothesis and are tiny; the minute Wilcoxon
p-values reflect n = 1477 paired, not effect size. This was initially filed as a sixth null.

*AOZ identified the mechanism and it was then confirmed.* For jointly normal (L,R), writing
U = (L+R)/2 and V = (L-R)/2, min = U - |V|; U and V are independent and |V| is uncorrelated
with every linear function of (L,R), so the best linear predictor of the minimum is exactly
U and

: R^2(rho) = (1 + rho) / [ (1 + rho) + (1 - rho)(1 - 2/pi) ]

which is *monotone increasing* in rho: 0.733 at rho = 0, 0.892 at 0.5, 0.961 at 0.8. Higher
partner correlation therefore means less nonlinear content, and because the structured parts
cancel in the difference, noisier switching. Both observed effects follow if true partners
are more correlated.

*The mediation test settles it.* Regressing linear R^2 on arm, partner correlation and
dominance across all 2,954 observations:

| arm coefficient, unadjusted                          | +0.0408 (p = 6.2e-05) |
| arm coefficient, adjusted for correlation + dominance| +0.0047 (p = 0.50)    |

*88.4% of the arm effect is accounted for, and what remains is indistinguishable from
zero.* Nothing about being a true pairing predicts the minimum's nonlinearity once
correlation is controlled. Empirically cor(rho, linear R^2) = +0.516, positive as the
theory requires. The bivariate-normal model predicts a gap of +0.0157 against the observed
+0.0408: right sign and mechanism, magnitude underestimated about 2.6x, as expected from
non-normal fields.

*Two honest caveats for any writeup.* The correlation effect is modest (median paired
difference 0.0224 Pearson); the significance comes from n = 1477. And there is a large
shared baseline: even random pairs sit at Spearman 0.325, so all these gene fields share a
dominant common spatial mode, most likely "is there tissue here". The pairing effect rides
on top of that and must not be quoted as if 0.365 were pairing-specific. The Pearson to
Spearman gap (0.11 against 0.36) also says these relationships are strongly monotone but
non-linear on the raw scale, which argues for a rank-based operator.

* Questions answered this session, so they are not re-asked

- *"n draws" in the Seurat depth table* is the number of independent random feature subsets
  of that size that were clustered. 18 rather than 19 at depth 50 because one array task
  died on a transient lmod module-cache race, which is missing-at-random and harmless.
- *Max depth.* Full depth is 1,449. The Seurat random arm was capped at *1,000* (depth 1300
  blew the =future.globals.maxSize= cap and was cut); the substitute random arm reached
  1,300. Neither ran a random subset at full depth, which is definitionally impossible since
  at 1,449 there is only one subset. *Consequence:* the random curve and the paper's
  full-depth number are never directly comparable, so do not write "a random 1,000 beats all
  1,449" (0.1199 against 0.1237 is about 1.3 standard errors, across different setups).
- *Is the sd in that table reducible by more draws? No.* It is the genuine between-subset
  spread, a property of the data. More draws shrink the standard error of the mean
  (sd/sqrt(n); 19 to 99 buys a factor of 2.3), not the sd. Crucially, the "is the ordering
  special" question compares one fixed value against the null *spread*, so the denominator
  is the sd and more draws cannot resolve it; they would only sharpen the percentile from
  1/19 to 1/99. Evidence that the spread is mostly real rather than annoy approximation
  noise: it falls 3.6-fold from depth 10 to 1,000, whereas approximation noise would be
  roughly flat. *Recommendation: do not spend compute topping up the draws.*

** The mass-action operator is already implemented AND already run

=calc.conv()= in =code-spatial-smoothing/raw-counts/03-array-post-process.R= supports
="min"=, ="prod"=, ="geom.mean"= and ="kinetic"=. The kinetic branch solves the correct
receptor-ligand equilibrium (verified algebraically: r_e = E +/- sqrt(E^2 + r*k.d) with
E = (r - l - k.d)/2, returning bound concentration r - r_e).

Affinities k.d = 0.01, 0.5 and 1 were computed on both observed counts (=m.data.*=) and
modeled estimates (=m.est.*=), each with and without ligand diffusion (=m.est.d.*=). With
min, product and geometric mean that is roughly *24 representations*, and Seurat assays for
many sit in =sam_sandbox/HD Embeddings/*assay.2025-07-25.Robj=. *None was ever reported.*

*Why this is structurally important.* As k.d approaches 0 the kinetic operator converges to
the common minimum; as it grows large it converges to the product over k.d. So the minimum
and the product are the two limits of one physically motivated family indexed by binding
affinity, not three competing arbitrary choices. The operator comparison the project has
been agonizing over is largely computed already; what is missing is clustering and scoring
each representation under a defensible criterion.

* The one piece of new work Option 3 needs

*The Jensen result. Identified, computable, not yet run.* The pipeline computes
=min(E[L], E[R])=. The right quantity is =E[min(L,R)]=. The minimum is concave, so by
Jensen's inequality the published interaction fields are *biased upward, and biased most
where the posterior is widest*, which is exactly where a conservative answer is wanted.
Posterior standard deviations are saved (see above), so no refits are needed, and for
approximately Gaussian independent marginals there is a closed form. One computation, one
figure (bias magnitude in space, and bias against posterior sd), one paragraph.

* Where the win-criteria thread stands, now re-scoped

The cross-validated predictive fit measure is *built and calibrated* and belongs to Paper 2.

=cv-fit-measure.R= scores a partition by how well it predicts held-out gene expression in
spatial blocks it was not fitted on: 16,530 genes never used to build the fields, 70
contiguous blocks of 20x20 bins across 10 folds (blocks because the fitted correlation
length is about 10 bins), per-domain mean profiles with shrinkage, Poisson deviance,
reported as deviance explained against a global-mean null. Baseline on the published
20-domain partition is 0.17459.

Calibration by label surgery: arbitrary splits of pure domains -0.00039/-0.00023/-0.00024;
informative splits along reference boundaries -0.00006/+0.00042/+0.00194; merges
-0.00014/-0.00036/-0.00337; boundary jitter at 10% and 25% -0.00113/-0.00333. Merges and
jitter behave and jitter is monotone; informative splits exceed arbitrary ones in all three
pairs with no overlap.

*The operating rule, which took care to establish.* The absolute score varies by sd 0.00035
across fold assignments, larger than most calibration deltas. But comparisons are paired on
the same folds, so fold noise cancels. Paired across 8 fold assignments: arbitrary split
mean -0.00042 sd 0.00010; informative split mean -0.00010 sd 0.00005; separation 0.00032,
or 3.8 pooled sd, using the *weakest* informative split. *Always score partitions on shared
folds and compare deltas; never compare absolute scores across fold assignments.*

Read comparatively, not as an absolute goodness score: two of three informative splits still
lower the score, they just cost about a quarter of what an arbitrary split costs.

*Not established:* that it separates the six experimental arms. It was calibrated on one
partition with single-domain edits. Paper 2 should pre-register a check: run two arms first,
confirm separation exceeds the noise floor, then commit.

*Trap:* a first noise estimate returned exactly zero variance across six fold seeds because
sourcing the core script re-ran its own =set.seed(1)=. Source once, then redraw folds in
place.
